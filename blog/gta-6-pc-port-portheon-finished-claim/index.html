Portheon says its GTA 6 PC port is finished, but nothing is shown

By GTA6 PC EditorialPublished November 28, 2026

Project Portheon, one of the community projects working on an unofficial GTA 6 PC port, says its work is finished. In a Discord message shared on social media on November 27, 2026, developer Marcelo Miyake wrote that his work on GTA VI is now finished and he is just waiting for the green light to release it alongside the other developers. No build has been released, no footage has been published, and no independent test exists, so the claim is unverified, and it sits in the same category as every other unproven port claim this week.

Short answerProject Portheon developer Marcelo Miyake says his GTA 6 PC port work is finished and he is waiting for approval from the other developers before releasing it, according to a Discord message shared on X on November 27. Two days earlier the project claimed a private build was fully playable at a stable 30 FPS. No build, footage or independent test has been published, trackers mark the claim unverified, and PORTHEON is a real launcher for unofficial UFC game ports, not a proven GTA 6 project. Any download carrying the Portheon name should be treated as a scam.

What was claimed

On November 27, 2026, a screenshot of a Discord announcement began spreading on X. It was attributed to Marcelo Miyake, a developer on Project Portheon, and shared by the account MadeByNik. The message reads: "My work on GTA VI is now finished. I'm just waiting for the green light to release it alongside the other developers." The same message says the developer is taking a break to return to his Portheon project for new UFC updates, mods and performance fixes, and it promotes a new Telegram channel for anonymous posts and confidential link sharing.

GameRiv reported the announcement the same day, noting that Miyake provided no release date and that the project's claims have yet to be independently verified. The post by MadeByNik drew hundreds of thousands of views, with replies split between excitement and doubt, and several replies pointing out that no build or footage exists. A separate Facebook post resharing the same screenshot drew the same split: some readers hopeful, others dismissing the announcement as fake or premature, citing GitHub trackers and the lack of evidence.

The claim from two days earlier

The November 27 message was not the project's first claim. On November 25, GameGPU reported that the Portheon developers had announced their closed internal build was fully playable and ran stably at 30 frames per second, and that the team intended to bring the final version to the highest possible level of optimisation before releasing it. The report says the developers promised to publish all the materials soon, with no specific release date.

The same report adds useful context. The team had previously released several forks targeting graphics cards with 8 GB of video memory, and experimental builds that achieved around 6 FPS on select graphics cards. The developers said none of those preliminary builds reflect the actual state of the final port, and urged people not to judge the release by them. That is a fair point on its own, but it also means the only numbers attached to Portheon come from the project itself, with nothing shown in public.

Put the two claims together and the story is this: a private build that is fully playable at 30 FPS on November 25, and finished work waiting for a release decision on November 27. If both claims were true, Portheon would be the furthest unofficial port of the week, ahead of the public builds that top out at about 20 FPS. But a claim is not a build, and nothing has been shown to support either one.

What Portheon actually is

One part of the story checks out. PORTHEON is a real project. It is a launcher on GitHub for unofficial PC ports of EA's UFC games, with releases as recent as this month, and Miyake is the developer behind it. That gives the name weight: this is not a project invented yesterday, and the developer has shipped unofficial ports before.

What does not check out is the GTA 6 part. No GTA 6 build from Portheon exists on any public page, no footage of a Portheon GTA 6 build has been published, and no tracker lists a Portheon build among the projects it has measured. The independent tracker VGNW, which cross checks the fan run trackers, the projects' own measurement logs and news reports, puts the best credible frame rate at about 20 FPS, with about 15 projects having got the game world on screen. Portheon is not among the verified ones.

The modemguides timeline, which rates each port claim by its evidence, gives the Portheon claim its lowest rating: one out of six, for a relayed social post with no build, footage or independent test. The same timeline notes that the Discord server could not be opened, so the screenshot's authenticity cannot be confirmed, and that replies under both X posts dispute the claim.

Why the claim is unverified

Verification needs something to check, and Portheon has provided nothing. The public builds that have been verified all share the same pattern: a repository or a release that outsiders can open, footage with a visible frame counter, and a tracker or an outlet that reproduces the result. VIctory has all three, which is why its 20 FPS figure is treated as credible. ScarlettPort has a public release and outlet reported tests. Portheon has a Discord screenshot.

The screenshot itself is the weakest form of evidence in this story. It is a picture of a message, not a link to a server anyone can open, and the outlets that tried could not open the Discord server to confirm it. The message also announces a Telegram channel for confidential link sharing, which is exactly how unofficial releases are usually distributed, but it is also exactly how fake downloads are distributed. A Telegram channel is not a release, and a promise to publish materials soon is not a publication.

There is also a timing problem. The project says its build is fully playable at 30 FPS, which would make it the best unofficial port by a clear margin, yet it has chosen not to show it, while every other project in the race publishes footage the moment it has anything to show. That does not prove the claim is false. It does mean the claim should be treated as unproven until something is shown, because the projects that are real show their work.

The pattern to watch for

The biggest risk in this story is not whether Portheon is real. It is what happens next, when scammers use the Portheon name. A release announced through Discord and Telegram is the pattern scammers copy first, and the Portheon message itself tells followers to watch a new Telegram channel for links. Anyone searching for a Portheon GTA 6 download in the next few days will find pages claiming to have it, and the odds are that those pages are fake.

The fake download problem is already documented. ESET warned on November 13, 2026, about fake GTA 6 launchers and installers, including one called gta6_installer.exe that carried the Vidar password stealer. Bitdefender reported on November 20 that a fake GTA VI PC client installs an information stealer in the background, targeting browser logins, cookies, Discord, Steam, crypto wallet data and clipboard contents. On November 26, Bitdefender documented two fake GitHub pages advertising GTA 6 for Windows that told visitors to paste a command into PowerShell, a trick known as ClickFix. Our fake download warning explains how to spot these files.

So the rule is simple. Do not download anything that claims to be the Portheon build, do not paste commands from a website into Run, PowerShell or Command Prompt, and do not disable your antivirus for any GTA 6 download. If Portheon ever releases a real build, it will be reported by outlets and trackers that can test it, and we will cover it here.

How it compares to verified progress

The verified picture is very different from the Portheon claim. The best credible frame rate across all ports is about 20 FPS, with 19 to 23 FPS logged in light scenes and 6 to 13 FPS in the city, even on the most powerful cards. About 15 projects have got the game world on screen, and in at least 6 of them you can control the character. None of them is stable, and none of them is a game you can play through. Our November 28 update has the full picture, including the removal of Belobug and the disappearance of CGBJ.

Portheon's claim of a fully playable build at a stable 30 FPS would put it far ahead of that verified picture, which is exactly why it needs proof. A 30 FPS fully playable build would be the story of the week, and it would be verifiable in an afternoon by any tracker with the build. The fact that no tracker has it, and no footage exists, is the whole story.

There is a precedent for caution. Project Leonida, the biggest port project of the launch week, had its Discord server shut down and its GitHub repository removed after a Take-Two DMCA notice dated November 24, which named the repository and more than 2,900 forks. Its creator said he was banned from Discord for two years and is quitting the effort. Unofficial ports live under legal pressure, and a project that announces itself loudly without showing anything is either about to be tested, or about to be copied by scammers. Either way, the right response is to wait for proof.

What would count as proof

Three things would change the status of the Portheon claim. A public release that a tracker or an outlet can download and run. Footage with a visible frame counter that can be checked against the known game. Or logged frame rates on named hardware, reproduced by someone outside the project. Any one of those would move Portheon from unverified to credible, and a combination would settle it.

Until then, the honest status is unverified, not finished. The developer may have finished his work, and the build may exist on his own machine, but a finished private build is not a port anyone can play, and a Discord message is not a release. PC players waiting for GTA 6 should watch the trackers, not the screenshots, and should treat every download that claims to be the Portheon build as a scam. When there is a real PC version, Rockstar will announce it, and we will cover it here.

Quick answers

Is the Portheon GTA 6 PC port real?

Unverified. PORTHEON is a real launcher for unofficial ports of EA's UFC games, but its GTA 6 claim rests on an X post and a Discord screenshot. No build, footage or independent test has been published as of November 27, 2026. Treat any download carrying the Portheon name as fake until a build is public and independently tested.

Who is behind Project Portheon?

The developer named in the announcement is Marcelo Miyake, who builds PORTHEON, a launcher for unofficial PC ports of EA's UFC games, with releases as recent as this month. His GTA 6 work is described as a team effort with other developers, but no other developer has been named, and no public GTA 6 build from the project exists.

What did Portheon claim on November 25?

On November 25 the project claimed its closed internal build was fully playable and ran stably at 30 frames per second, and said it would publish all the materials soon. The same report notes the project's earlier experimental builds ran at about 6 FPS, and that the team released forks for graphics cards with 8 GB of video memory. None of those builds has been shown publicly.

Why has nobody verified the claim?

Because there is nothing to verify. No build has been released, no footage has been published, and no tracker or outlet has run the project. The Discord server could not be opened by the outlets that checked, so the screenshot's authenticity is unconfirmed. Replies under the X posts that spread the claim dispute it, and trackers that mention Portheon mark it as an unverified claim with no evidence shown.

Could a Portheon download be a scam?

Yes, and that is the main risk. A release announced through Discord and Telegram is the pattern scammers copy first, and the Portheon message itself promotes a new Telegram channel for sharing links. Security researchers have documented fake GTA 6 PC downloads carrying password stealing malware, and fake GitHub pages posing as community ports. Do not download anything that claims to be the Portheon build.

When will we know if Portheon is real?

Only when a build is public and independently tested. What would count as proof is a public release that a tracker or an outlet can run, with logged frame rates on named hardware, or footage that can be checked against the known game. Until then the claim is a Discord message, and the honest status is unverified, not finished.
