GTA 6 PC mod menu and launcher explained: Viper trainer and the Suco launcher

By GTA6 PC EditorialPublished November 27, 2026

Three days after the missing GTA 6 Xbox executable surfaced, the unofficial PC port effort grew its first tools. A mod menu called Viper and a dedicated PC launcher were both demonstrated on November 26, 2026. Neither makes the game playable, and neither is something you should download, but together they show how fast the fan effort is moving.

Short answerOn November 26, 2026, community developers demonstrated the first unofficial GTA 6 PC mod menu, a trainer called Viper, and a dedicated PC launcher. Both run on top of unfinished unofficial builds: the launcher demo averaged about 10 fps, and the fastest community forks run at about 20 fps with visual bugs. There is still no official PC version, no public release of these tools, and nothing safe to download.

What the Viper trainer is

GameGPU reported on November 26, 2026 that a demo of the first working mod menu for the unofficial GTA 6 PC port had appeared online. The menu is called Viper, and the published video shows part of a story mission in a swampy area, with the player opening the trainer interface on screen and testing its functions during the mission.

Viper's menu is split into five main categories: vehicle spawning, character stat management, vehicle customization, weapon assignment, and game world customization. GameGPU reports that the trainer's database already recognizes 642 vehicle models and 52 types of weapons, and the player section lists options for health, invulnerability, ammunition, stamina, and speed. The community noted that the cutscene in the demo was likely sped up to look smoother, and some environmental objects still loaded with delays.

Separately, footage shared by X user cccurrenccc shows an in-game mod menu running on an unofficial GTA 6 PC build. TweakTown and Notebookcheck both covered the clip, and both note that the interface resembles mod menus built for GTA 5 over the years. The current implementation was created mainly for debugging, not for playing the game.

The Suco launcher

The same day brought a second tool. Brazilian streamer Suco demonstrated a dedicated launcher for the unofficial PC version of GTA 6. Notebookcheck reports that the build in the demo averaged around 10 frames per second and occasionally dropped to just 2 fps, despite running on a system with an AMD Radeon RX 7900 XTX graphics card and 64 GB of system memory.

A launcher sounds like a small thing, but it matters for this project. Until now, running the unofficial builds has meant manual setup and command line work. A launcher that starts the game and manages its files is the kind of tool that appears when a project stops being an experiment and starts being something other people are expected to run. It is still early, and the performance in the demo shows how far there is to go, but the direction is clear.

Why modders build tools before the game works

It looks strange to build a mod menu for a game that barely runs, but there is a practical reason. GameGPU notes that the ability to load any vehicle, weapon, or effect model at a single point speeds up the search for graphical errors and lets developers benchmark the stability of the compatibility layers that render the Xbox files. Spawning a vehicle is faster than playing to the point where one appears. Testing every weapon is faster with a menu than by finding them in the world.

That is also why the first menu is a trainer, not a gameplay mod. Trainers exist to change game state on demand, which is exactly what developers need while the port is broken. The Viper menu is a debugging tool that happens to look like a cheat menu, because the two jobs use the same controls.

CGBJ published a launcher, then its repository went dark

The fastest project in the race had a confusing day of its own. MakeTechEasier reports that CGBJ published a Windows x64 launcher and runtime in a public GitHub repository early on November 26, 2026, at 06:35 UTC, with a release recording the build and a checksum. The repository and the release page returned a 404 error on a later check. The cause is unknown, and as of the community tracker's check at 00:21 UTC on November 27, GitHub had published no takedown notice naming the repository. PortWatch moved CGBJ back to its list of private builds the same day, and a pinned note described the code as taken down temporarily.

XDA Developers reported that it was unclear whether the team made the project private to avoid a potential takedown notice or whether Rockstar's legal department acted. Our November 26 update covered CGBJ reaching 91 percent completion at 25 fps in its own tests, and a packaged build claiming to be CGBJ circulating on torrent sites. That torrent build is unverified, and the tracker warns that downloads offered as GTA 6 for PC have carried malware.

Do not download any build, launcher, or trainer claiming to be a GTA 6 PC port. No version of Viper has a confirmed public release, the CGBJ packages on torrent sites are unverified, and fake installers using the port race as bait are already circulating. The only safe position is to watch and wait.

Where performance stands

The tools arrived while the frame rate story kept moving. TweakTown reported on November 26 that community builds on VIctory, the open-source compatibility layer that runs GTA 6's Xbox code directly on Windows, have jumped to around 20 fps. PC Games Hardware reported that a public fork called VIctory-NVIDIA averages 20 to 22 fps indoors on an RTX 5070 and a Ryzen 7 9800X3D, though it uses the Xbox Series S graphics profile, rendering internally at 960 by 540 before upscaling, with ray tracing largely disabled. Footage posted to X by MrPoulpy shows a jet ski sequence at 19 to 23 fps on the coast and about 12 fps in town, with missing shoreline water and magenta streaks on a road. TweakTown's author wrote that he could not independently verify who made the build or what hardware it ran on, so treat the clip as a claim with video attached, not a benchmark.

The main VIctory project itself is still far behind its forks. Its README, updated November 25, still lists the first mission intro at 1 to 2 seconds per frame. Another fork, AvatarManz's VIctory-7900XTX, ran for 41 minutes without a crash, with indoor scenes at 13 to 20 fps and the first boat mission at 19 to 22 fps on a Radeon RX 7900 XTX. Our PortWatch guide explains how the community tracker scores each project, and our release date page tracks the official position separately: Rockstar has announced no PC version.

The legal pressure has not stopped the work

All of this is happening under legal fire. Take-Two filed a DMCA notice on November 24 that took down the Leonida project's network of 3,007 GitHub repositories, and TweakTown notes that several other port repositories have since vanished without a public takedown notice. Leonida itself is still down, and its leads have left the project. Our crackdown explainer has the full timeline.

The mod menu and launcher do not change the legal picture. They are built on the same leaked Xbox files, and Take-Two has shown it will act against public repositories. The work continues in private forks and smaller groups, which is also why so many of the claims now come with no public code to check. Treat every frame rate and every demo as a report from the project itself until a build is public and independently tested.

The bottom line

November 26 was the day the unofficial GTA 6 PC effort grew tools, not just frames. A trainer, a launcher, and a published runtime all appeared within hours of each other, which is what a maturing project looks like. But none of it is a game you can play. The fastest builds are private or unverified, the public code keeps disappearing, and the only official GTA 6 PC position is still silence from Rockstar. Watch the tools, download nothing.

Quick answers

What is the GTA 6 PC mod menu?

It is a trainer called Viper, the first unofficial mod menu for the fan-made GTA 6 PC port, demonstrated on November 26, 2026 in footage shared by X user cccurrenccc. Its interface resembles GTA 5 mod menus, and it was built mainly for debugging, with five categories covering vehicle spawning, character stats, vehicle customization, weapon assignment, and world customization.

Can I download the Viper trainer?

No. No public release of Viper has been confirmed. Only demo footage has been shown. Anything offered as a Viper download is unverified and should be treated as a likely scam or malware risk.

What is the Suco launcher?

It is a dedicated launcher for the unofficial GTA 6 PC build, demonstrated by Brazilian streamer Suco on November 26, 2026. In the demo the game averaged about 10 frames per second and occasionally dropped to 2 fps, on a PC with a Radeon RX 7900 XTX graphics card and 64 GB of RAM.

Does the mod menu mean GTA 6 is playable on PC?

No. The tools run on top of unfinished unofficial builds. The fastest community forks report about 20 fps with missing water, broken lighting, and other visual bugs, and the main VIctory project still lists its first mission intro at 1 to 2 seconds per frame. Rockstar has announced no official PC version.

What happened to the CGBJ launcher?

CGBJ published a Windows launcher and runtime in a public GitHub repository early on November 26, 2026, and the repository returned a 404 error on a later check. The cause is unknown, and GitHub had published no takedown notice naming the repository as of November 27. PortWatch moved CGBJ back to its list of private builds.
