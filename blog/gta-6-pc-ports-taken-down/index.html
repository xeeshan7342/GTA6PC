Every GTA 6 PC port taken down so far

By GTA6 PC EditorialPublished November 27, 2026

In the week since GTA 6 launched on consoles, the community projects trying to run the leaked Xbox build on PC have started disappearing. Some were hit by a Take-Two DMCA notice. Others vanished with no notice at all. This page keeps one running list of every project that has gone offline, and what is known about why.

Short answerAs of November 27, 2026, four GTA 6 PC projects tracked by PortWatch have gone offline: Leonida (deleted November 23, then 3,007 forks disabled by a Take-Two DMCA notice on November 24), americas-recomp (404 since November 23), ZETTROX (404 on November 25, account gone) and the private omonkey build (taken down November 25). Only Leonida has a published DMCA notice. Projects such as VIctory and XCL are still online.

Leonida: the only confirmed Take-Two takedown

Leonida was the biggest GTA 6 PC project by far, with more than 32,000 GitHub stars. On the night of November 23 its Discord server stopped resolving, its repository was deleted and its creator said he was stepping away, all within about an hour. At the time, nobody had published a legal notice.

That came the next day. Take-Two's first DMCA notice against a GTA 6 port appeared in GitHub's public DMCA log on November 24. Because Leonida's fork network was larger than 100 repositories, GitHub processed the notice against all of it: 3,007 repositories now show GitHub's "unavailable" page.

Take-Two's notice made three arguments. It said Leonida is a derivative work of GTA 6, that it breaks the reverse-engineering ban in Rockstar's terms of service, and that its "bring your own files" disclaimer is misleading, because at this point the files users must supply can only come from the leaked Xbox build. We covered the notice in more detail in Take-Two confirms the DMCA takedown that shut down Leonida.

The quiet disappearances

The other three removals are harder to read. ZETTROX was briefly one of the most talked-about builds, reported to run from Lucia's prologue into Jason's first mission without crashing. Then on November 25 both the repository and the developer's GitHub account disappeared. americas-recomp went offline two days earlier while its owner's account stayed active, which points to the owner pulling it rather than GitHub.

omonkey never had public code at all. Its progress was shown through live streams on a Rumble channel run by someone else, and its owner said it was taken down on November 25. Private builds like this are a growing part of the story, which we look at in why the furthest GTA 6 PC builds are private.

Without a published notice, we can't say whether Take-Two, a platform or the developers themselves were behind these. It is common for developers to delete their own work once a large project in the same space receives a legal notice.

Take-Two had already set the pattern with GTA V

None of this came out of nowhere. On November 18, the day before GTA 6 launched, Take-Two sent GitHub four DMCA notices against unofficial GTA V ports, including iOS, macOS and Android versions. Reports counted from 349 disabled forks of one project to more than 600 projects in total. Three of the four projects shipped no game files, and Take-Two argued that telling users to bring their own copy did not excuse them.

That is the same argument it used against Leonida, and every GTA 6 project on the PortWatch board uses a similar "use your own copy" line. Developers can file a counter-notice, which forces GitHub to restore the code unless Take-Two sues. Take-Two did exactly that over the re3 and reVC GTA III and Vice City projects in 2021.

Who is still online

VIctory, XCL and the builds forked from them are still up as of November 27, along with separately uploaded copies of Leonida. They are not GitHub forks of Leonida, so the November 24 notice did not reach them. PortWatch's own assessment is that the same argument could be applied to any of them. Our explainer Will Take-Two shut down every GTA 6 PC port? covers what could happen next.

What this means for you

The takedowns don't change the basics. None of these projects is a finished, legitimate way to play GTA 6 on PC, all of them depend on leaked game files, and re-uploads of removed projects are exactly the kind of thing scammers repackage with malware. If you want the official version, the PC release date page has the latest, and our scam guide explains how to spot fake downloads.
