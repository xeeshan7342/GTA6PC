Will GTA 6 on PC have ray tracing, DLSS and ultrawide support?

By GTA6 PC EditorialPublished November 23, 2026

GTA 6 is the best looking console game Digital Foundry has ever tested, and ray tracing is the reason why. PC players want to know whether that lighting, plus DLSS and ultrawide support, will carry over to the PC version. The console facts are confirmed. The PC features are not, but Rockstar's last PC release points clearly in one direction.

Short answerRay tracing is confirmed on consoles, not on PC. GTA 6 uses full ray-traced global illumination and reflections on every console, with ray-traced shadows on PS5 Pro. No PC version has been announced, so no PC graphics features are confirmed. Based on GTA V Enhanced, which added ray tracing, DLSS, FSR and ultrawide support to PC, the PC version of GTA 6 is very likely to offer the same, but that is an expectation, not a fact.

The short answer

Ray tracing is already a core part of GTA 6, but only on consoles so far. Digital Foundry's tech review calls it arguably the best implementation of ray tracing it has ever seen, with full ray-traced global illumination and ray-traced reflections on every console version, and ray-traced shadows added on PS5 Pro. None of that has been confirmed for PC, because Rockstar has not announced a PC version at all. Our GTA 6 PC release date page tracks that story.

The reason to be optimistic is Rockstar's own recent history. When GTA V Enhanced came to PC on March 4, 2025, it brought ray-traced global illumination and ambient occlusion that were exclusive to PC, plus ray-traced shadows and reflections, Nvidia DLSS 3, AMD FSR 1 and 3, DirectStorage, ultrawide support and DualSense adaptive triggers. Rockstar treated PC as the best version of the game, not an afterthought. If it follows the same playbook for GTA 6, the PC version should offer the same set of features, and probably more. That is an expectation based on a real precedent, not a confirmed fact.

What the consoles already do with ray tracing

Digital Foundry tested GTA 6 for more than 100 hours across all four console versions, and the ray tracing findings are the same across the board. Every version uses ray-traced global illumination and ray-traced reflections, on smooth and rough surfaces alike. The base PS5 and PS5 Pro render at 1440p internally, as does Xbox Series X, while Xbox Series S renders at 720p and upscales to about 1080p using FSR 4. All four target 30 FPS, and Rockstar has confirmed there is no 40 or 60 FPS mode on console.

The PS5 Pro is the only console with ray-traced shadows, which Digital Foundry says remove the coarse cascade boundaries of traditional shadow maps and produce natural penumbra transitions and pin-sharp contact shadows. The base PS5 generally holds 30 FPS during routine play but can dip into the mid-20s during intense driving and prolonged explosions. The Series S is the weakest version, with frequent dips into the mid-20s in dense combat. Our console frame rate guide covers those numbers in full.

What matters for PC players is that the lighting system is already built around ray tracing. This is not a game where ray tracing is a toggle added on top of a baked lighting setup. The global illumination, the reflections in wet streets and windows, and the shadows are ray traced at the core of the engine. A PC version would not need to add ray tracing. It would need to expose the settings that are already there, and scale them up.

What Rockstar gave PC players last time

GTA V Enhanced is the best guide to what Rockstar does when it brings a console-era game to PC properly. The update landed on PC on March 4, 2025, almost three years after the same features reached PS5 and Xbox Series X|S, and it added more than the console versions had. The full list, as Rockstar announced it, was ray-traced ambient occlusion and global illumination only available on PC, ray-traced shadows and reflections, AMD FSR 1 and 3, Nvidia DLSS 3, faster loading with DirectStorage, enhanced support for higher resolutions, higher aspect ratios and higher frame rates, DualSense controller support with adaptive triggers, and enhanced audio with Dolby Atmos.

Two details from that release are worth remembering. First, Rockstar said frame generation support for DLSS and FSR was planned for a future date, which shows it thinks about upscaling as a core PC feature, not a checkbox. Second, Rockstar did not add HDR support to GTA V Enhanced, which reviewers noted as a disappointment. So the precedent is strong but not perfect: expect ray tracing, DLSS, FSR and ultrawide, but do not assume every modern feature will make the cut.

There is also a timing lesson. GTA V Enhanced took nearly three years to reach PC after consoles. The reported plan for GTA 6 on PC is much faster, with GamesBeat reporting a first quarter 2027 target, but that report is unconfirmed and Rockstar has not commented on it. A faster PC release would still need the same graphics work, and Rockstar has now done that work once already with the same engine family.

What to expect from GTA 6 on PC

Everything in this section is an expectation, not a confirmed feature. No PC version has been announced, and no PC graphics settings have been published. With that said, here is what the evidence points to.

Ray tracing should be the headline PC feature. The console versions already run ray-traced global illumination and reflections, so the PC version should offer those same effects with quality settings, plus the ray-traced shadows that only PS5 Pro has today, and possibly ray-traced ambient occlusion, which was PC exclusive in GTA V Enhanced. Expect individual toggles for each effect, as GTA V Enhanced offered, rather than a single on or off switch.

DLSS and FSR support is very likely. Ray tracing is expensive, and upscaling is how modern PC games pay for it. GTA V Enhanced shipped with DLSS 3 and FSR 1 and 3, and Rockstar talked about frame generation as a planned addition. For GTA 6, expect DLSS and FSR at launch, with frame generation either at launch or in a later patch. The console versions do not use temporal upscaling, relying on spatial upscaling or PSSR instead, so PC upscaling would be a genuine step up in image quality.

Ultrawide support is very likely too. GTA V Enhanced added support for higher aspect ratios and resolutions beyond 4K, and ultrawide monitors are now standard equipment for PC players. A PC version of GTA 6 that launched without ultrawide support would be a surprise. The same applies to high refresh rates: the console game is locked to 30 FPS, but a PC version should let the frame rate run free on capable hardware. Our system requirements guide explains what kind of hardware that is likely to take, with estimates only.

Two more features from the GTA V Enhanced list should carry over: DirectStorage for faster loading on NVMe SSDs, and DualSense adaptive triggers for players using a PlayStation controller on PC. Neither has been confirmed for GTA 6, but both are now part of Rockstar's standard PC toolkit.

What this means for your graphics card

Until Rockstar publishes official requirements, nobody can tell you which card will run GTA 6 with ray tracing at a given frame rate. The console evidence does set a rough floor. The game runs ray-traced global illumination and reflections on console hardware comparable to a mid-range PC graphics card, at 1440p and 30 FPS, with upscaling doing heavy lifting. A PC aiming for 60 FPS with ray tracing at 1440p will need meaningfully more power than that, and DLSS or FSR will matter more than raw raster performance.

Do not buy a graphics card for GTA 6 yet. Wait for Rockstar's official requirements, then buy to those numbers. Our PC build guide gives budget, mid-range and high-end estimates for when that day comes, and we will update it the moment real requirements land.

Quick answers

Will GTA 6 on PC have ray tracing?

Not confirmed. Every console version uses ray-traced global illumination and reflections, and PS5 Pro adds ray-traced shadows, but Rockstar has announced no PC version and no PC graphics features. GTA V Enhanced added full ray tracing to PC, so it is likely, not guaranteed.

Will GTA 6 on PC support DLSS?

Not confirmed, but likely. GTA V Enhanced added Nvidia DLSS 3 and AMD FSR 1 and 3 to PC, and DLSS is the standard way to offset the cost of ray tracing. Expect DLSS and FSR support, but wait for Rockstar to confirm it.

Will GTA 6 on PC support ultrawide monitors?

Not confirmed. GTA V Enhanced added support for ultrawide monitors, higher resolutions and higher frame rates on PC. Ultrawide support is likely for GTA 6 on PC, but nothing has been announced.

Will GTA 6 on PC run at 60 FPS?

Unknown. Every console version targets 30 FPS, and Rockstar has confirmed there is no 40 or 60 FPS mode on console. A PC version would not be capped the same way, but the frame rate you get will depend on your hardware and settings.

What graphics card will GTA 6 PC need for ray tracing?

Unknown. Rockstar has published no PC system requirements, so any card recommendation today is a guess. Our system requirements guide has estimates only, and we will update it when Rockstar publishes the real numbers.
