GTA 6 PC port update, November 26: CGBJ hits 91 percent and 25 fps

By GTA6 PC EditorialPublished November 26, 2026

The unofficial race to run GTA 6 on PC made its biggest jump yet on November 26, 2026. The leading project, CGBJ, reached 91 percent completion and averaged 25 frames per second in its own tests, close to the 30 fps the game targets on consoles. The same day, a build claiming to be CGBJ appeared on Russian torrent sites, and the project's public code went private again within hours. Nothing here is an official PC version, nothing is independently verified, and nothing is safe to download. But the numbers moved further in one day than they had in the whole week before.

Short answerOn November 26, 2026, the fan project CGBJ reached 91 percent completion and averaged 25 frames per second in its own tests, with a boat mission demo averaging 27 fps on a Ryzen 7 9800X3D and RTX 5070 Ti. AvatarManz sits second at 83 percent and 16 fps, and Belobug third at 8 fps with ray tracing. CGBJ published its code publicly on the morning of November 26 and made it private again within hours, and a build claiming to be CGBJ appeared on Russian torrent sites the same day. No build is verified, none is playable in the normal sense, and the official position is unchanged: there is no PC version of GTA 6.

What changed on November 26

On November 25, the story was a 77-minute session at about 5.2 fps and forks claiming 9 to 12 fps. On November 26, the numbers jumped. DSOGaming reported that CGBJ, the most advanced project, reached 91 percent completion and can run the game at an average of 25 fps. That is close to the 30 fps the game targets on consoles, though DSOGaming stresses the frame rate is not stable yet. The same day, the fan tracker that scores every public port project moved its top answer from a number to a word: it now answers the question "Does GTA VI run on PC yet?" with "Mostly".

The jump is real, but keep it in proportion. Every figure in this article comes from the projects themselves or from outlets reporting their claims. Nothing has been independently tested by a second party, and the fastest build is private. Our November 25 update covers where the race stood a day ago, and our Portwatch guide explains how the tracker scores each milestone.

CGBJ at 91 percent: the numbers behind the claim

GameGPU published the most detailed test of the day. A team calling itself Group BoYoo showed a boat mission on open water with correct water rendering, reflections, daylight, clouds, a bridge on the horizon, a minimap and story subtitles. Across the demo, performance stayed between 25 and 30 fps, averaging 27 fps, with minimums of 21 to 22 fps and frame times of 33.4 to 40.7 milliseconds. The test machine used an AMD Ryzen 7 9800X3D processor, an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5070 Ti graphics card and 32 GB of RAM. CPU use sat at 66 to 70 percent, GPU use at 80 to 85 percent, the game itself used 15.2 to 15.4 GB of RAM with about 27 GB total system use, and video memory use was 11.9 to 12.0 GB of 14.1 GB available.

Two caveats matter. First, open water is one of the lightest scenes in the game, and the developers have not shown the same performance in downtown Vice City, where the crowds and traffic load the CPU hardest. Second, the demo is the project's own footage on its own machine, not an independent benchmark. Community users quoted by GameGPU noted the speed of the progress, from 2 fps with serious graphical errors to a stable 25 to 30 fps in a few days, and also noted the open water caveat. DSOGaming's editor goes further and predicts CGBJ will reach 100 percent completion by tomorrow, which would mean the full campaign at an average of 30 fps without visual glitches. That is a prediction, not a fact, and we label it as one.

AvatarManz and Belobug: the rest of the leaderboard

Behind CGBJ, the race has a clear order. AvatarManz's fork of VIctory sits second at 83 percent completion and runs at 16 fps, according to DSOGaming. Busy street chases drop it to 6 to 10 fps, while walking outdoors runs at about 11 to 17 fps. Belobug's VIctory fork, previously in first place, is now third at 8 fps, but it has ray tracing working, which no other build has shown. TweakTown adds a fourth data point: a fork called VIctory-NVIDIA averages 20 to 22 fps indoors on an RTX 5070 and a Ryzen 7 9800X3D, though it uses the Xbox Series S graphics profile, rendering internally at 960 by 540 before upscaling, with ray tracing largely disabled. Footage of a jet ski sequence shows 19 to 23 fps on the coast and about 12 fps in town, with a visible gap in the shoreline water.

The frame rate is only half the story. The other half is stability and completeness. The November 25 leader, klbf-lab, reported a 77-minute session with no crash at about 5.2 fps. The November 26 builds are faster but their longest sessions are shorter, and the tracker still lists memory leaks and missing features across the board. A fast demo is not a finished game.

The build went public, then private, then torrent sites

The most confusing part of the day is what happened to CGBJ's code. DSOGaming reports the team pushed a public version a few hours before its article, then decided to make it private again. TalkEsport, citing Russian outlets, reports the project's GitHub repository disappeared less than an hour after it went public. The same day, Russian sports outlet Championat reported that a user posted a first build of the port to torrent trackers, and Mail.ru's Hi-Tech section reported the same release. Posts on X claim the torrent build runs at 25 to 30 fps, but TalkEsport says the claims have not been independently tested, and the outlet has not downloaded the files and will not link to them.

Mail.ru also listed requirements quoted for the CGBJ build: a Ryzen 5 3600 or Core i5-10400 processor, 32 GB of RAM, a graphics card with 16 GB of video memory and 209 GB of SSD space. Those are the project's own quoted requirements, not official specs, and they have not been verified. The torrent appearance is exactly the outcome our earlier reporting warned about: once a build is public for even an hour, copies spread to places the team cannot control. Take-Two filed its DMCA notice against Leonida on November 24, and several other port repositories have since vanished without a public takedown notice, according to TweakTown. Leonida itself is still down, its leads have left, and no official PC announcement has followed.

Do not download any build claiming to be a GTA 6 PC port. The torrent copies are unverified, they are built on leaked Xbox files, and fake installers using the port race as bait are already circulating. The only safe position is to wait for a public release and independent tests.

What the tracker says now

The community tracker now lists 39 port projects, according to a technical write-up on the Russian community site Habr cited by TalkEsport. The same write-up says around 80 percent of the story is playable on the CGBJ build, while rival projects sit at around 16 fps and 8 fps. A video summary published on November 26 puts the same picture in plainer terms: on Monday a PC drew its first frame of GTA 6 and it was black, and by Wednesday night one fan's code ran the game at up to 20 fps, 22 out on the water, for 41 minutes without a crash. Its notes add that the tracker's top score was taken down temporarily that morning, and that the figures had not been independently reproduced.

The honest summary is that the race is real, the progress is fast, and the finish line is still unconfirmed. A private build at 25 fps is not a PC version, and a torrent copy is not a release. Rockstar's own GTA VI page still lists PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S only, and no PC version has been announced. Our release date page tracks the official position separately.

The bottom line

November 26 was the day the port race stopped looking like a curiosity and started looking like a countdown. CGBJ at 91 percent and 25 fps, AvatarManz at 83 percent and 16 fps, and a tracker that now says "Mostly" are all genuine steps. But the build is private, the public copy lasted less than an hour, the torrent versions are unverified, and the densest parts of Leonida are still untested. If CGBJ reaches 100 percent and goes public, that will be the day to pay attention. Until then, watch the tracker and download nothing.

Quick answers

Is GTA 6 playable on PC now?

No. The fastest unofficial build, CGBJ, is private and reports about 25 fps on average in its own tests, with no stable public release. No official PC version exists, and no unofficial build counts as playable in the normal sense of the word.

What is CGBJ and how far has it got?

CGBJ is a private fan project built on the VIctory compatibility layer. DSOGaming reported on November 26, 2026 that it reached 91 percent completion at an average of 25 fps. GameGPU reported a boat mission demo averaging 27 fps on a Ryzen 7 9800X3D and an RTX 5070 Ti, with minimums of 21 to 22 fps.

Can I download the CGBJ build from torrent sites?

No. Builds claiming to be CGBJ appeared on Russian torrent sites on November 26, but nothing about them is verified, and TalkEsport reports the claims have not been independently tested. Torrent builds of leaked games are a known malware route, and downloading leaked game files carries legal risk. Wait for a public release and independent tests.

Why did CGBJ go private again?

The team published a public version on the morning of November 26 and made it private again within hours, according to DSOGaming and TalkEsport. No public reason was given. Take-Two's DMCA action against the Leonida project on November 24 is the obvious context, but that link is our reading, not something the team has confirmed.

When will a GTA 6 PC port be playable?

Unknown. DSOGaming's editor predicts CGBJ could reach 100 percent completion within a day, but that is a prediction, not a fact. The community tracker still answers "Mostly", not "Yes". Until a build is public and independently tested, treat every frame rate claim as a report from the project itself.
