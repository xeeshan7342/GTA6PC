GTA 6 boots on PC: what modders actually got running

By GTA6 PC EditorialPublished November 24, 2026

The missing piece arrived on November 23, and modders moved fast. Within about three hours of Cyberleek releasing the GTA 6 Xbox executable, the game was booting on a Windows PC. It reached the settings screens and played audio, but no gameplay. That is a real milestone with a narrow meaning, and it does not make GTA 6 playable on PC.

Short answerCyberleek released the missing GTA 6 Xbox executable on November 23, 2026. Within about three hours, modders booted the game on a Windows PC, reaching the initial settings screens with in-game audio but no graphics and no gameplay. There is still no legitimate way to play GTA 6 on PC.

What happened on November 23

Cyberleek released the missing main executable for its leaked GTA 6 Xbox build on Monday, November 23, 2026. The group had promised the file for that day, after first tying its release to the market value of its own crypto token. TechSpot and TweakTown both report the file is now circulating, with SHA-256 checksums provided so people can check the file against the version being passed around.

The release completed the leak Cyberleek started on launch day, November 19, when it posted what it called a near-complete Xbox build of GTA 6, about 209 GB and labeled version 1.0.2.1, but held back the executable. Without that file there was nothing to run. With it, modders had the last missing piece, and they moved fast. Full background is in our leak explainer and our earlier executable update.

Booted on PC in about three hours

Within about three hours of the executable appearing, modders had the game booting on a Windows PC. TweakTown reports the build reached the initial settings screens and startup notices, and TechSpot reports footage posted on X by a user called Chasm showing the game booting from a Windows desktop, reaching the initial settings menu and playing in-game audio, with no graphics rendered.

That is a real milestone, and it is also a narrow one. The game boots. It does not play. No gameplay areas are reachable, no world loads, and nothing playable has been shown. Notebookcheck, which tracked the same footage, reports the build gets through the initial setup and then stops there.

Modders are using AI tools, including Anthropic's Claude, to reverse-engineer the code and work out what blocks the game past those screens, according to TweakTown. The remaining work is the hard part: the Xbox build calls console-only system functions that Windows does not have, and those have to be rebuilt or faked one by one.

How long until it is playable?

Nobody knows, and the estimates going around are guesses, not schedules. TweakTown cites a post from Chasm estimating one to three days to get into the game, and two to four weeks before higher frame rates are possible on high-end graphics cards. Treat those as one person's estimate, not a timeline.

The honest version is shorter: booting to a settings screen is the first step, not the last one. Getting from a menu to a playable open world, with working graphics, audio, input and saves, is a much bigger job, and every project working on it is unofficial, unpaid and now under legal pressure. Our crackdown report covers what happened to the main port project the day after the boot.

What Cyberleek said, and what it wants

Cyberleek attached a message to the executable saying it is fighting against features locked behind paywalls and paid upgrades, including the $20 upgrade to the Ultimate Edition, according to Notebookcheck. TechSpot reports the same message framed the leak as a stand against digital-only releases, extra charges for content and selling licenses as purchases.

The group has also promoted its crypto token throughout the leak. TechSpot reports Cyberleek allegedly cashed out an estimated $270,000 from the token in August, and again in October. The token tie-in was part of the executable release too: Cyberleek had said it would release the file sooner if the token reached a $15 million market cap.

What this does not change

There is still no legitimate GTA 6 on PC. The leaked build is not a product you can buy, and it is not a shortcut to playing the game. Downloading leaked game files carries legal risk, and fake installers using the leak as bait are already circulating. Our download scams guide explains the risks in detail.

The official PC version is a separate question. A GamesBeat report from October says Rockstar plans a PC release in the first quarter of 2027, but Rockstar has not confirmed it. Our release date page tracks that story. Nothing in this leak changes Rockstar's plans, and Rockstar has not said the unofficial port work will speed up an official announcement.

Quick answers

Did GTA 6 boot on PC?

Yes, within about three hours of the executable being released on November 23, 2026. Modders got the leaked Xbox build to boot on a Windows PC and reach the initial settings screens. No gameplay areas are reachable and nothing playable has been shown.

Did Cyberleek release the GTA 6 executable?

Yes. Cyberleek released the missing main executable for the leaked GTA 6 Xbox build on November 23, 2026, as it had promised. The file is circulating with SHA-256 checksums so people can check it against the version being passed around.

Can I play GTA 6 on PC now?

No. The build only reaches the settings screens, with no graphics and no playable areas. There is no legitimate GTA 6 PC download, and the official PC version has not been announced.

Is the leaked GTA 6 PC build safe to download?

No. Leaked game files carry legal risk, and fake installers using the leak as bait are already circulating. Downloading leaked files can also put your PC and accounts at risk.

Will the unofficial port speed up Rockstar's official PC release?

There is no evidence for that. Rockstar has not commented on the unofficial port work, and the reported first quarter of 2027 window for the official PC version is still unconfirmed.
