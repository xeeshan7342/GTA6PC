What is AnyXboxX Neptune, and how far has it got with GTA 6 on PC?

By GTA6 PC EditorialPublished November 26, 2026

AnyXboxX is the quietest project near the top of the GTA 6 PC port race. It is not a fork chasing frame rates in public. It is a prototype Xbox Series X emulator that builds on VIctory's code, and on November 25 it moved to a packaged edition it calls Neptune. Its own README reports 14 to 17 frames per second in the game world on an RTX 4070 SUPER, with a median of 13.7. Nothing about it is playable yet, and two outside runs disagree with its numbers, but it is the only project in the race that approaches the problem as emulation first.

Short answerAnyXboxX is a prototype Xbox Series X emulator built on VIctory's code. Its Neptune edition reports 14 to 17 fps in the game world on an RTX 4070 SUPER, with a median of 13.7. It is in-game but not playable, it needs the leaked Xbox build, and its own README says a 32 GB machine is short of memory.

What AnyXboxX is

Most of the projects in the GTA 6 PC port race are compatibility layers. VIctory, the project that started the race, builds a fake Xbox around the leaked Xbox build: a loader, shims for the console libraries a PC does not have, and a fake Direct3D device that records draw commands and replays them through Vulkan. Leonida took the recompilation route, translating the game's code so it runs natively. AnyXboxX is different from both. Notebookcheck describes it as a prototype Xbox Series X emulator, and that is the honest label. It can run console code directly on the host CPU, because the Xbox Series X uses the same x86-64 instruction set as a PC, or inside the Unicorn CPU emulator in an isolated mode.

That approach gives it a different set of problems. Before the missing executable surfaced, AnyXboxX inventoried the leaked package and found 103 of the 104 expected files, with only the main executable missing. Its shader work so far covers a Microsoft ray-tracing library shipped with the game rather than the game's own shaders. Its research notes add that the game's mesh shaders carry no root signatures, the tables that map shaders to their resources, and that those sit inside the missing executable. In other words, the emulator could see the files before it could run them, and some of what it needs to draw the game correctly was locked inside the one file Cyberleek held back.

What changed on November 25: Neptune

On November 25, AnyXboxX moved to a packaged edition of VIctory it calls Neptune. The tracker that reads each project's README every 15 minutes puts it plainly: AnyXboxX builds on VIctory's code, is kept in its own repository, and moved on November 25 to a packaged edition it calls Neptune. The tracker adds that Neptune's author has no public contact, which is worth knowing, because it means nobody outside the project can ask what changed or check the packaging.

The README updated at 23:53 UTC on November 25 reports about 14 to 17 frames a second in the game world on an RTX 4070 SUPER with its default settings, a median of 13.7 over 7 minutes and 13.8 over automated 13-minute runs without a crash. A mode that favours the picture over speed runs at about 7 to 9. Those are the project's own measurements on its own machines, and the tracker notes them as such. Two outside runs are on record and neither is verified: a reader's 12-minute run on an RTX 4090 with earlier settings measured a median of 2 that rose to about 7 in its last two minutes, and a launcher project's 10-minute run of an earlier build on an RTX 4060 with 8 GB reported about 14. The gap between those two runs is the whole story of this race in miniature: same project, different machines, different builds, different numbers.

Two things stand out. First, AnyXboxX is the fastest NVIDIA result in the table that comes with a clean no-crash run attached, even if the run is only 13 minutes. Second, it is the only project in the leading group that runs on Linux as well as Windows, and on AMD cards as well as NVIDIA, which matters for anyone hoping this work spreads beyond one graphics card.

The memory problem

The README is blunt about the cost. It says a 32 GB machine is short of memory and a 12 GB card runs out of texture room, with dips to 1 to 6 frames a second while the world streams in. That is the number that decides whether a frame rate is real. A median of 13.7 over 7 minutes means little if the game stalls to 1 fps every time you drive into a new district.

It is not alone. Vice-Tay, another fork rebuilt on November 26, says the frame rate fell from about 20 to under 10 as the game's memory grew from 33 to 52 GB, past the 43 GB its PC has. The pattern is consistent across the race: these builds hold tens of gigabytes in memory, and a normal gaming PC with 16 or 32 GB of RAM and a 12 GB card is below what they need. AnyXboxX saying so in its README is more useful than the headline frame rate, because it tells you what to buy if you want to reproduce the result, and it tells most readers they cannot.

What the picture looks like

The tracker rates AnyXboxX at picture 3 of 5: lit in light and shade, with visible faults. Its words are even stand-in light indoors, with some parts missing. The stand-in light is the same compromise every leading fork makes. The game's ray-traced lighting does not run in these builds, so the projects fake the ambient light and the picture looks right in sunlight and wrong in shadow. DexNom RayTime is the one exception, and its ray tracing costs it nearly all of its speed. The mode that favours the picture on AnyXboxX runs at about 7 to 9 fps, which is the trade in one number: prettier, slower.

Why the emulator route matters

There is a reason to watch an emulator even while the compatibility layers post faster numbers. A compatibility layer has to reimplement every Xbox system call the game uses, one shim at a time, and it breaks whenever the game uses a call nobody has shimmed yet. An emulator has a different ceiling: if it can run the console's code faithfully, the game should behave like it does on the console, including the parts nobody has looked at yet. The price is speed, because emulation does more work per frame, and accuracy, because the Xbox GPU speaks a dialect of graphics that PC cards do not.

Neptune is a step toward the middle of that trade. It is a packaged edition of VIctory, which means AnyXboxX is now building on the compatibility layer's work instead of duplicating it, while keeping its own emulator approach. If the packaging holds, the project gets VIctory's fixes for free and keeps its own way of running the code. If it does not, it inherits VIctory's bugs too. The tracker will show which one it is within days, because it reads the README every 15 minutes.

What to watch next

Watch for a second machine reproducing the 14 to 17 fps figures, which would turn AnyXboxX from a claim into a baseline. Watch for a fix to the memory problem, because 32 GB being short is the number that keeps this out of reach of normal PCs. And watch for a 30-minute session at 20 fps, the tracker's playable bar, which is the first number that would mean someone actually played the game rather than watched it run.

Watch Take-Two as well. The publisher has already had GitHub remove Leonida and 3,006 forks, and our Take-Two crackdown report has the full story. AnyXboxX is open-source code on GitHub, the same place Leonida lived, and the same notice could reach it. Until then, the honest summary is the one the tracker prints at the top of its page: playable, no. The emulator fork is in the game, it is fast for its approach, and it still needs leaked files that nobody can legally download.

Do not download anything claiming to be GTA 6 for PC. There is no legitimate download, and fake installers using the leak as bait are already circulating. The unofficial port projects are open-source code on GitHub, not game downloads, and they need leaked files to run.

Quick answers

What is AnyXboxX?

AnyXboxX is a prototype Xbox Series X emulator that builds on VIctory's compatibility-layer code. It can run console code directly on the host CPU or inside the Unicorn CPU emulator in an isolated mode. On November 25, 2026 it moved to a packaged edition of VIctory it calls Neptune.

How fast does AnyXboxX run GTA 6?

Its README reports about 14 to 17 frames per second in the game world on an RTX 4070 SUPER with default settings, a median of 13.7 over 7 minutes and 13.8 over automated 13-minute runs without a crash. A mode that favours the picture runs at about 7 to 9 fps. Two outside runs are on record and neither is verified.

What PC do you need to run AnyXboxX?

More than most people have. Its README says a 32 GB machine is short of memory and a 12 GB graphics card runs out of texture room. The frame rate also dips to 1 to 6 fps while the world streams in. The project is tested on Windows and Linux, on NVIDIA and AMD cards.

Is AnyXboxX playable?

No. The PortWatch tracker rates it 2 steps short of its playable bar, which is 20 frames per second with 30 minutes without a crash. It is in-game at stage 6 of 7, with even stand-in light indoors and some parts missing.

Can I download AnyXboxX and play GTA 6 on PC?

No. AnyXboxX is open-source code, not a game download, and it needs the leaked Xbox build to run at all. There is no legitimate GTA 6 download for PC, and sites offering one are scams.
