GTA 6 PC port ScarlettPort goes public: first release reaches 20 FPS

By GTA6 PC EditorialPublished November 27, 2026

For the first time since GTA 6 launched on consoles, an unofficial PC port of the game has a public release. ScarlettPort version 0.5.2 boots GTA 6, passes the menus and legal screens, and reaches the open world on a Windows PC, running at a median of about 19 to 22 frames per second on an AMD Radeon RX 9060 XT. It is a real milestone for the port race, and it is still a long way from a game you can actually play.

Short answerScarlettPort 0.5.2 is the first unofficial GTA 6 PC port with a public release, reported by DSOGaming on November 27, 2026. It boots to the open world, with a median frame rate of 18.9 to 22.0 FPS on an AMD Radeon RX 9060 XT, and adds mouse and keyboard support plus direct DualSense and DualShock 4 input. It is built on the leaked Xbox build, it is not stable, and it is not an official release. Rockstar has announced no PC version, and most files circulating as a GTA 6 PC download carry malware.

What ScarlettPort is

ScarlettPort is one of the community projects trying to run GTA 6 on Windows by translating the leaked Xbox Series build into a native Windows program, a technique called static recompilation. Until now, every project in this race required building the code yourself, which kept them out of reach for most people. DSOGaming reports that ScarlettPort is the first to break that barrier with a public release, so it can be run directly instead of compiled from source.

That makes it the most accessible port so far, and the most watched. The Mac Observer, reporting the same day, confirms that several unofficial ports have now reached interactive gameplay on Windows, with character models, textures, controller inputs and even ray-traced lighting working. ScarlettPort is the furthest along in making that progress available to try. Our November 26 port update covered the race the day before, when the best builds were still stuck at building from source.

What version 0.5.2 actually does

The latest version boots GTA 6, passes the menus, the save screens and the legal screens, then reaches the game world. Version 0.5.0 took 178 seconds to get there. Once in the world, the game shows lit rooms, ambient and sky lighting, and full textures, and the player can control the character in the opening area. On-demand level loading now works as intended, so the world streams in instead of stalling.

Version 0.5.2 also fixes a black screen that could appear after loading with the Stable preset. The engine now uses GTA 6's own upscaler, falling back to it when the AMD upscaler is not present, and it removes a 500 millisecond wait per frame while the game builds pipelines on its first runs. The update restores 40 shader variants, fixes blending for integer targets, and brings back the voxel visibility shader, with shader work that includes fixes from the DexNom and VIctory projects. Another fix stops the game from reusing a shader size query that had caused AMD's compiler to crash on 29 compute pipelines.

Performance: about 20 FPS, not 60

Tests on an AMD Radeon RX 9060 XT show a median frame rate of 18.9 to 22.0 FPS, according to DSOGaming. That is the honest number to remember, and it lines up with the independent tracker, which puts the best credible frame rate across all ports at around 20 FPS in light scenes, dropping to 6 to 13 FPS in the city. The Mac Observer puts most experimental builds even lower, at 1 to 5 FPS on powerful hardware, which shows how far ahead ScarlettPort is, and how far all of them still have to go.

Do not believe clips claiming 60 FPS or 4K at 120 FPS. The tracker notes that most of those videos are console footage passed through a PC, not the game running on PC at all. A real port at 20 FPS with crashes is the current state of the art. Anyone promising a smooth 60 FPS download is selling a fake, and the fakes usually carry malware. Our GTA 6 PC download scams guide explains how to spot them.

Controls and the launcher

Version 0.5.2 adds better input options. Players can now turn on a PC-style mouse and keyboard layout with mouse look, which is the first time a port has felt like a PC game to control rather than a console game running through a translator. DualSense and DualShock 4 controllers can also be read directly by the port.

The launcher got its own set of changes too. It now shows live shader progress all the way to 100 percent, leaves two CPU cores free for the desktop, and has a new update banner for installing the latest version. The log also gives a clear message when the game runs out of memory, and player logs show more GPU details, with shader progress updating every 100 shaders.

What it is not

ScarlettPort is not an official release, not a finished game, and not a stable way to play GTA 6. No community project has demonstrated a stable, fully playable version, and the Mac Observer says so plainly. The port is built on files leaked from the Xbox version, and Take-Two is actively removing these projects. A DMCA notice published by GitHub on November 24 took down Project Leonida and its network of 3,007 repositories, and the same enforcement can reach any project, including this one.

There is also a practical warning. DSOGaming notes that, like other recompiled ports, ScarlettPort contains no game assets of its own, so it needs a copy of the game to run at all. Anything offered online as a complete GTA 6 PC download, a single installer, or a torrent of the full game is not this port. It is almost certainly a fake, and the fakes circulating now commonly carry password-stealing malware. If you want to know whether the game is playable on PC today, our is GTA 6 playable on PC page has the short answer: not in any way you should trust.

What comes next

The other big project in the race, CGBJ, is still not public. DSOGaming reports that a build claiming to be CGBJ is already on torrent sites, and advises avoiding it, since torrent sites are currently filled with fake GTA 6 PC torrents. Modders are already experimenting with a multiplayer mode on the closed beta build of CGBJ, and a clip claiming 30 FPS multiplayer circulated on November 27, but that claim is unconfirmed, and our multiplayer video explainer has the full picture.

The bigger question is still the official one. Rockstar has announced no PC version, and the GamesBeat report pointing to the first quarter of 2027 remains unconfirmed. Until Rockstar speaks, ScarlettPort is the clearest sign of how far the community has come, and how far it still has to go: the open world, at 20 FPS, with crashes, on borrowed files, one takedown notice away from disappearing.

Quick answers

What is ScarlettPort?

ScarlettPort is an unofficial PC port of GTA 6 built by static recompilation from the leaked Xbox Series build. Version 0.5.2, reported on November 27, 2026, is the first of these ports to have a public release, so it can be run without building the project from source. How fast does ScarlettPort run GTA 6?

Does ScarlettPort support mouse and keyboard?

Yes. Version 0.5.2 adds a PC-style mouse and keyboard layout with mouse look. DualSense and DualShock 4 controllers can also be read directly by the port, instead of going through a compatibility layer.

Is it safe to download ScarlettPort or any GTA 6 PC port?

Be careful. These ports are built on leaked files, Take-Two is actively removing them, and most files circulating as a GTA 6 PC download carry password-stealing malware. We do not link to downloads. The only safe way to play GTA 6 on PC is to wait for Rockstar to announce and release an official version.

Is there an official GTA 6 PC version?

No. Rockstar has announced no PC version of GTA 6, and the game is only on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X and S. A GamesBeat report pointing to the first quarter of 2027 remains an unconfirmed rumour.
