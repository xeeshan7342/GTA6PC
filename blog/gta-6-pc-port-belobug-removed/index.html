Belobug removed: GitHub deletes the leading GTA 6 PC port and its author account

By GTA6 PC EditorialPublished November 28, 2026

The fastest unofficial GTA 6 PC port is gone from GitHub. Belobug, the AMD fork of VIctory that led the PortWatch VI tracker at 91.3 percent toward playable, stopped answering on November 27, 2026. By the next morning both the repository and the author account returned 404 errors. It was not a DMCA takedown, and the code had already spread further than GitHub.

Short answerOn November 27, 2026, GitHub removed the Belobug repository and the Belobug author account. PortWatch VI reported at 18:33 UTC that GitHub removed them for a Terms of Service violation, not a DMCA takedown, and no public GitHub notice names the project. Belobug led the tracker at 91.3 percent toward playable, with a reported 22 frames per second. VIctory-NVIDIA now leads the board at 90.0 percent and about 20 frames per second. There is still no official PC version and no legitimate download.

What happened on November 27

Belobug was a fork of VIctory, the open-source project that runs GTA 6's Xbox code on Windows, tuned for AMD graphics cards. By November 26 it led the PortWatch VI tracker at 91.3 percent toward playable, ahead of every other project on the board, with story progress rated around 80 percent. Then it vanished. The public repository stopped answering on November 27, 2026, and a check on November 28 found both the repository address and the author's GitHub profile returning 404 errors.

PortWatch VI reported the removal in a news post timed 18:33 UTC on November 27, saying GitHub had removed the repository and the author's account for a Terms of Service violation, and that it had confirmed the removal. Playground.ru reported the same thing, citing PortWatch. The Belobug project site, which tracks the fork, confirmed both addresses were still returning 404 on November 28.

It is the second big disappearance in four days. Take-Two's DMCA notice on November 24 disabled Leonida and 3,007 related repositories. Belobug is different: it is the first leading project to go down without a DMCA notice behind it.

A Terms of Service removal, not a DMCA takedown

The distinction matters, because it decides what paper trail exists. When GitHub acts on a DMCA notice, it publishes the notice in its public log, which is how the Leonida takedown could be verified the same day. GitHub does not publish a notice for a Terms of Service removal. That is why no public GitHub document names Belobug, and why the Belobug project site is careful not to call the removal a copyright takedown.

What is confirmed is the outcome, not the trigger. The repository is gone, the account is gone, and PortWatch says GitHub removed them for a Terms of Service violation. Whether GitHub acted on a complaint or on its own review is not public. Take-Two has not claimed the removal, and no notice connects it to the November 24 DMCA action.

The pattern around Belobug is not unique. The ZETTROX project and its author account also went to 404 on November 25 with no published notice, and a comparison site noted on November 27 that the ZETTROX author's whole account had stopped loading a day earlier. Removals without public notices are becoming a regular feature of this race, which makes tracker reports and direct checks the only way to confirm what happened.

The two frame rates, and why both are reported

Belobug leaves behind a confusing pair of numbers, and they are worth keeping separate. The project's own README, in a section dated November 25, 2026, said the fork was not playable yet. On the one PC it named, a Windows 11 machine with a Radeon RX 9070 XT, a Ryzen 7 9800X3D and 64 GB of RAM, the open world ran at 5 to 8 frames per second, with menus at 30. That is the project's own measurement, in its own words, and it is the conservative figure.

The faster number, about 22 frames per second on average without crashes, comes from Playground.ru citing PortWatch, and it was repeated by PCQuest, which added that the build used about 30 GB of system memory and 12 GB of video memory. PortWatch's leaderboard also carried the 22 figure. It describes a later private build, not the build the README measured, and nobody outside the project has independently tested it. Both numbers stay in the record because they describe different builds at different times, and collapsing them into one would hide that.

The same caution applies to the tracker score itself. The 91.3 percent is PortWatch's own scoring, built from the project's README, commits and stream footage, with self-reported claims labelled as such. A second tracker, GTA6 Recomp, scored the same project at 62 percent on its own scale. Scores are a tracker's opinion, not a benchmark.

Who leads now

With Belobug off the ranked board, VIctory-NVIDIA takes first place on PortWatch at 90.0 percent, running at about 20 frames per second. The private CGBJ build follows at 89.6 percent, with the best reported frame rate on the board at about 25 frames per second, but it is private, so nobody outside its group can test it. Tracker channels citing PortWatch also report that AvatarManz, the AMD fork ranked at 81.8 percent, had its repository and account removed the same day as Belobug.

The public projects that remain online are VIctory itself and XCL, the two open-source layers most of the private builds sit on. Our takedown tracker lists every project that has gone offline so far, and our PortWatch guide explains how the tracker scores each project. The board changes daily: Belobug led it by a single day, and its removal moved the leader without a single line of code being written.

Does a removal kill the project?

Not necessarily. When CGBJ went public briefly on November 26, it gathered 293 stars and 82 forks before its page went dark, and several forks stayed active. Code that has been forked, starred and streamed does not disappear when one repository does. Private groups and smaller forks keep working, which is also why so many of the claims now come with no public code to check.

What a removal does kill is verification. While Belobug was public, anyone could read its README, check its commits and measure its claims. Once the repository is gone, the only record is what trackers and reporters saved before it vanished. That is why the 22 frames per second figure can no longer be checked against the code, and why the README's 5 to 8 figure is now the last measurement the project itself published.

Do not download anything offered as the Belobug build. The public repository is gone, the project has no official download, and packages using the port race as bait have carried malware. The only safe position is to watch and wait.

The bottom line

Belobug was the closest thing to a playable unofficial GTA 6 PC port, and GitHub removed it and its author's account on November 27 without a public notice. The removal was reported as a Terms of Service action, not a Take-Two DMCA takedown, and the distinction is real: one leaves a paper trail and the other does not. VIctory-NVIDIA now leads the tracker at 90.0 percent, and the race goes on in private forks, as it has after every removal so far. Rockstar has announced no official PC version, and nothing about this changes that.

Quick answers

What happened to Belobug?

GitHub removed the Belobug repository (Belobug/VIctory-Belobug-AMD) and the Belobug author account on November 27, 2026. Both addresses returned 404 errors when checked on November 28. PortWatch VI reported the removal was for a Terms of Service violation, not a DMCA takedown.

Was Belobug taken down by Take-Two?

No public evidence says so. Take-Two's DMCA notice of November 24 targeted Leonida and its fork network. No DMCA notice names Belobug, and GitHub publishes notices for DMCA removals but not for Terms of Service removals. PortWatch reported the Belobug removal as a Terms of Service action by GitHub.

How fast was Belobug?

Two different numbers are reported, and they measure different things. The project's own README, dated November 25, said the world ran at 5 to 8 frames per second on a Radeon RX 9070 XT, with menus at 30. Playground.ru, citing PortWatch, reported about 22 frames per second on average without crashes in a later private build. The README figure is the project's own measurement, the 22 figure is a tracker report.

Who leads the GTA 6 PC port race now?

VIctory-NVIDIA leads the PortWatch VI board at 90.0 percent and about 20 frames per second after Belobug was removed. The private CGBJ build sits behind it at 89.6 percent with a reported 25 frames per second. Tracker channels citing PortWatch also report that AvatarManz, ranked at 81.8 percent, was removed the same day.

Can I still download Belobug?

No. The public repository is gone, and there is no legitimate download. Copies circulating online are unverified, and packages offered as a GTA 6 PC download have carried malware. We do not link to any of them.
