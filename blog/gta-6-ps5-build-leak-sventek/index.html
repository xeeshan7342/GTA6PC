GTA 6 PS5 build leak explained: the Sventek dump, jailbreak video and PC impact

By GTA6 PC EditorialPublished November 27, 2026

Six days after GTA 6 launched on consoles, a second console build entered the leak story. A hacker going by Sventek claims a complete PlayStation 5 build of GTA 6, with its executable decrypted, leaked on November 25, 2026. Videos that surfaced the same day appear to show the game running on a jailbroken PS5, and the files are now circulating on piracy networks. Nothing about the dump has been independently confirmed, and neither Rockstar nor Sony has commented.

Short answerOn November 25, 2026, a hacker called Sventek claimed to have leaked a complete GTA 6 build for PlayStation 5, with the executable file (eboot) decrypted. The archive is reported at about 191 GB and unpacks to more than 500 GB. Videos show the game apparently running on a jailbroken PS5 on firmware 11.60, even though the retail game requires firmware 14.23. The claim is unconfirmed: no outlet has independently verified the files, and the dump gives PC players no legitimate way to play the game.

What Sventek claims leaked

The claim arrived on November 25, 2026, from a hacker going by the name Sventek. In a message that spread through GTA 6 leak communities, Sventek said a torrent carried the full PlayStation 5 version of the game, with a note that the build was put together in a hurry so some files in the sce_sys folder might be missing, but that the entire game was dumped and the eboot, the executable file, was decrypted. The ixbt.games report on the claim puts the archive at around 191 GB, unpacking to more than 500 GB, while Notebookcheck describes a 540 GB download circulating on piracy networks. The sizes differ between reports, which is itself a sign that nobody outside the leak scene has handled the files directly.

Sventek is not the same source as Cyberleek, the leaker behind the Xbox build that surfaced on launch day. The PS5 claim is separate, and it is not attributed to Cyberleek. Our Cyberleek explainer covers the Xbox leak and the federal subpoenas Take-Two has used to hunt that leaker, while this story is about a different person, a different console build, and a different set of claims.

The jailbreak videos

On the same day the claim appeared, videos surfaced that seemed to back it up. One 18-second clip shows a car driving through dark Leonida streets at night. Another shows the game installed on a jailbroken console's dashboard, with a debug menu open. Notebookcheck reports that the footage identifies the console's firmware as 11.60, and Reddit users have since verified that files matching the description are being shared on piracy networks.

The firmware number is the part that does not add up yet. The retail version of GTA 6 requires PS5 firmware 14.23, and the public jailbreak scene has generally worked on older versions: the popular Relapse kernel exploit only works up to firmware 13.60, and before launch a researcher called Gezine claimed a game dump on firmware 14.22 while noting that a full jailbreak was not yet possible. Notebookcheck states plainly that it is unclear how hackers managed to boot the game on firmware 11.60. VGTimes adds that neither the authenticity of the videos nor the legitimacy of the alleged build has been independently confirmed. A video of a game booting does not prove the dump is complete, that saves work, or that the same method works after a restart or a game update.

Why the PS5 files matter to the PC port race

The Xbox build has driven every unofficial PC port effort so far, because it was the first complete build with a public executable. A PS5 build gives those efforts a second source of game data to study and compare. XDA Developers treats the dump as a second file source for PC port work, pointing at the KytyPS5 emulator, which is available on Windows and Linux and supports the eboot file structure. Our AnyPS5 guide explains where PS5 emulation of GTA 6 stands today, and our PS5 build on PC page covers the earlier firmware 14.22 dump and why a dump alone is not a playable game.

That said, a second pile of files does not solve the hard problems. The Xbox-based ports are still the furthest along, and even the fastest of those reach only about 20 frames per second with missing water, broken lighting and frequent crashes. An emulator route would face the same performance wall plus asset streaming, the problem of getting a 500 GB game fed through an emulator fast enough to play. The PS5 dump is interesting to developers. It is not a shortcut to a playable PC version.

The piracy problem, and the fake downloads

Take-Two has already shown how it responds to this scene. On November 24 it used a DMCA notice to take down the Leonida project and 3,007 GitHub repositories, and our crackdown explainer has the full timeline. A PS5 dump circulating on torrent trackers is the same kind of stolen material, and it will draw the same response. Reports also note an odd detail: the build circulating is said to be the Australian and New Zealand version, which carries censorship around drug use, so even as a pirated copy it is not the full uncensored game.

For everyone else, the practical warning is the same as it has been since launch day. There is no legitimate GTA 6 download of any kind, for PS5 or for PC. The files on piracy networks are stolen game data, and the installers and launchers offered in search results and social posts are mostly password stealers and crypto miners. Our download scam guide lists the red flags. Watch the story, download nothing.

Where this leaves PC players

The honest summary is short. A second console build is claimed to be loose, videos show it running on modified hardware, and the claim is unverified. Rockstar has announced no PC version, and the rumoured early 2027 window from GamesBeat remains a rumour. The PS5 dump may help developers compare files and test the KytyPS5 route, but it does not put GTA 6 on PC, and it does not change the wait for an official announcement. The only safe conclusion, as one outlet put it, is that unofficial claims are circulating around a leaked PS5 build while the supported version remains consoles only.

Quick answers

What is the GTA 6 PS5 build leak?

Hacker Sventek claims a complete GTA 6 build for PlayStation 5 leaked on November 25, 2026, with the executable file decrypted. The archive is reported at about 191 GB and unpacks to more than 500 GB. Videos show the game apparently running on a jailbroken PS5. The claim has not been independently confirmed.

Can I download the leaked GTA 6 PS5 build?

No legitimate download exists. The files circulate on piracy networks as a roughly 540 GB package, and downloading them would mean handling stolen game files. Fake installers using the leak as bait are also circulating, and many carry malware. Do not download anything offered as a GTA 6 PS5 or PC build.

How can the game run on PS5 firmware 11.60 when retail needs 14.23?

That is the unexplained part of the claim. Videos show a debug menu identifying firmware 11.60, while the retail game requires firmware 14.23. How the dump boots on the older firmware has not been explained or verified, which is one reason to treat the footage as a claim rather than proof.

Does the PS5 dump mean GTA 6 will run on PC soon?

Not directly. The dump is a second source of game files alongside the leaked Xbox build, and XDA Developers notes it could feed PC port efforts through the KytyPS5 emulator, which runs on Windows and Linux. But no emulator runs GTA 6 today, and performance and asset streaming remain major hurdles. There is still no official PC version.

Is the leaked PS5 build the uncensored version?

According to reports of the circulating files, the build is the Australian and New Zealand version, which carries censorship around drug use. That detail comes from community reports, not from Rockstar, and it has not been independently verified.
