Is GTA 6 playable on PC yet?

By GTA6 PC EditorialPublished November 26, 2026

Clips of GTA 6 running on PC are everywhere this week, and the headlines say the game is playable. The truth is narrower. A handful of unofficial builds can walk around Leonida on a PC, but at 1 to 5 frames per second, with broken lighting and no guarantee they will still be online tomorrow. Here is what playable actually means right now.

Short answerNo, not in any way you would want to play it. PortWatch VI says at least six unofficial builds have reached interactive gameplay, and ZETTROX cleared the prologue at 1 to 3 fps on an RTX 3050. That is a technical milestone, not a playable game. There is still no official PC version, and ZETTROX's own GitHub page vanished on November 25.

What "playable" means on the tracker

Most of the "playable" headlines trace back to one place: PortWatch VI, a fan-run tracker that monitors 35 unofficial projects trying to run GTA 6 on PC and reads each project's own notes every few hours. Its scale marks "Playable" at 50 percent and completing the game at 100 percent. A build counts as playable once it reaches interactive gameplay, which in practice means a controllable character walking around in the game world. Notebookcheck, reporting on the tracker on November 26, says at least six builds have reached that bar, and the most advanced have cleared Lucia's prison prologue and reached Jason's first mission, Gone Fishing.

That is a much lower bar than the word suggests. On the tracker's own milestone scale, reaching the main menu is 55 percent, entering the game world is 70 percent, and a stable 30 frames per second is 85 percent. A build can be "playable" on the tracker while running at a frame rate nobody would accept in a real game. The label measures progress, not quality.

ZETTROX: the furthest build, and the one that vanished

The build that pushed the furthest is called ZETTROX, an NVIDIA-tuned project that went public on November 25 at 01:12 UTC, built on the VIctory compatibility layer and a copy of it by another developer. According to its own notes and the tracker, both read on November 25, it ran for more than 90 minutes in one session without a crash, played through the whole opening chapter and got into the first mission, Gone Fishing. The tracker says it verified the game's first gun kill happening on PC hardware.

The numbers behind that milestone are sobering. The build ran at 1 to 3 frames per second on the developer's own PC, with an entry-level NVIDIA RTX 3050. Shadowed areas render solid black and bright scenes wash out to nothing. The first mission is reached, not finished. Nobody else has reproduced the result, and no news outlet has confirmed it independently.

Then ZETTROX disappeared. At 17:35 UTC on November 25, about 18 hours after the code went public, the project's GitHub page and the developer's account both returned "not found". No takedown notice has been published so far, so nobody outside the project knows whether it was a DMCA notice, a voluntary deletion or something else. Take-Two had GitHub pull down Project Leonida, the largest of these efforts, along with more than 3,000 copies of it, the day before, under a published DMCA notice. Our takedown report covers that notice in full.

ZETTROX is not the fastest build by reported frame rate. The leaderboard changed overnight as forks started combining each other's work, and the numbers now depend heavily on which machine and which scene was tested. Here is where the leading public builds reportedly stand as of November 26:

Read the table with care. The fastest numbers come from single machines that nobody else has reproduced, and different projects test different scenes: a quiet street is not a street chase. Notebookcheck puts the fastest builds at 3 to 5 fps overall, and one clip it cites shows the prologue at just 1 to 3 fps on a much stronger machine, an RTX 5070 Ti paired with a Ryzen 7 7800X3D and 32 GB of memory. A private build called CGBJ reportedly averages 25 fps in gameplay on an NVIDIA card with 16 GB of video memory, according to PortWatch, but its code is not public, so that claim is unverified. Our ray tracing report has the full leaderboard with the same caveats.

Why 1 to 3 fps is not playing

Smooth play starts at around 30 frames per second. At 1 to 3 fps, every second of game time takes 10 to 30 seconds of real time, and every input arrives long after you made it. The original VIctory build shows the gap clearly: it runs menus at 30 fps, but each frame in the game world takes one to two seconds. Getting to a menu and getting a stable frame rate with working graphics are very different milestones, and the projects themselves are the first to say so.

The picture is broken too. Without ray-traced lighting, which the console game uses throughout Leonida, shadowed areas render as flat black and bright scenes wash out. Textures arrive late or not at all, trees and far buildings flicker, and rooms are too dark. DexNom RayTime is the first public fork to report the game's ray tracing actually running on PC, at about 1 frame per second, which proves the code path works but costs most of the remaining speed.

What is still missing

Four things stand between these builds and a game anyone could actually play. First, performance: nothing public holds even 30 fps in busy scenes. Second, graphics: lighting, shadows and textures are incomplete in every public build. Third, verification: the fastest claims come from single machines, and the projects that publish their code invite anyone to check, which almost nobody has done yet. Fourth, stability of the projects themselves: Leonida is gone after a DMCA notice, and ZETTROX vanished within a day of going public, with no explanation published.

There is also a hard limit that no fork can fix. Every one of these projects needs the leaked Xbox build to run at all. None of them can use a copy you bought, because there is no PC copy to buy. They are compatibility layers standing in for the Xbox system, not PC versions of the game.

How to follow the race without getting scammed

The honest way to watch this is the tracker, not the clips. PortWatch VI scores every project against fixed milestones, from boot to a stable 30 fps, and our tracker explainer shows how to read its scores. Our November 25 update covers the day the forks passed 12 fps and Take-Two took down 3,007 repos.

Do not download a "GTA 6 PC" installer. The projects are source code and test builds, not downloads, and any site offering a ready-to-play PC version is a scam. PortWatch warns that copies and forks of the projects can contain malware, and security researchers have already found malware riding on the hype.

For now, the only way to play GTA 6 is on PS5 or Xbox Series X|S. The official PC version is reported to be targeting the first quarter of 2027. Check our release date page for updates.
