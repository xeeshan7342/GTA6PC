Why the furthest GTA 6 PC builds are private, and how to spot a fake stream

By GTA6 PC EditorialPublished November 27, 2026

If you have watched GTA 6 running on a PC this week, there is a good chance it came from a build nobody else can test. Several of the furthest-along unofficial builds have no public code at all. They exist as Rumble streams, X clips and claims. Here is what that means, and how to judge what you are watching.

Short answerSeveral unofficial GTA 6 PC builds, including darkworldhm, NotNozy, Doomsday and suri, have no public code and are shown only through streams on Rumble and clips on X. Some claim free roam at 12 to 15 fps, but most of their progress is self-reported, and PortWatch has verified only parts of it. A stream is not a download: anyone offering you a private build is very likely running a scam.

The builds you can watch but not test

Most GTA 6 PC projects that make the news have public code on GitHub, such as VIctory, XCL and the forks built on them. A growing group does not. These builds are shown off through livestreams and short clips, and their developers have not released anything others can run. PortWatch tracks them, but lists private builds without a rank because their progress can't be checked independently.

Why keep a build private?

None of these developers has explained it publicly, but the timing explains a lot. On November 24, Take-Two's DMCA notice against Leonida took down 3,007 GitHub repositories in one go, and other public projects disappeared in the days around it (see our takedown tracker). Code that was never published can't be taken down from GitHub. Streaming gameplay without releasing anything keeps the developer in the spotlight while lowering that risk.

The platform choice likely fits the same logic. Rumble is widely seen as more lenient than YouTube or Twitch, which tend to act quickly on publisher copyright claims. Even so, that is no guarantee: omonkey's build, which was streamed on Rumble, was taken down on November 25.

There is also a less charitable reason. A private build can't be checked, so its owner can claim more than it really does. That doesn't make every private build fake, but it means you should treat their claims as claims.

Several private builds come from public code

Private doesn't always mean built from scratch. In a November 25 report, PortWatch said several private builds that reach Jason's first mission, "Gone Fishing", appear to be built on hyphennn's victory-fork, a public fork of VIctory, with their own changes kept private. One group's multiplayer video ran on a private fork of CGBJ's build, which we covered in the GTA 6 PC multiplayer video. In practice, the public projects do much of the groundwork and the private builds add fixes on top.

How to tell a real stream from a fake

Fake GTA 6 PC footage was everywhere before these builds existed, and it hasn't gone away. Some checks that help:

  1. Long and unedited beats short and cut. Real builds at 3 to 15 fps look rough, stutter and crash. A smooth 30-second clip with music is more likely edited, or GTA V footage passed off as GTA 6.
  2. Look for the PC around the game. Real test streams usually show a desktop, a console window, a frame counter or a performance overlay.
  3. Check an independent tracker. PortWatch marks which claims it has verified and which are self-reported. A build no tracker has seen deserves extra doubt.
  4. Watch for money. Paid Discord access, "donate to unlock", crypto tokens or a download link in the chat are the clearest red flags. The Cyberleek leak itself came wrapped in paid votes and token goals.
  5. A stream is never a download. If a private build is real, its developer isn't selling it to strangers. Anyone offering you one is almost certainly distributing malware or running a scam.

The bottom line

Private builds are where some of the most impressive GTA 6 PC footage is coming from, but they are also the hardest to trust and impossible to use. Even the best of them run far below playable frame rates, and all of them depend on the leaked Xbox files. For the real thing, keep an eye on our GTA 6 PC release date page.
