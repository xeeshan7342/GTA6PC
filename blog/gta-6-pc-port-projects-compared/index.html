GTA 6 PC port projects compared: VIctory, XCL, ScarlettPort, AnyXboxX and the rest

By GTA6 PC EditorialPublished November 27, 2026

A week after GTA 6 launched on consoles, about 35 projects are trying to run it on PC, and a comparison published on November 27 tracks 12 of them in detail. They are not 12 different games. Most of them share the same base code, a project called VIctory, while one rival line, XCL, is built separately, and one project, ScarlettPort, is the first with a public release. Here is what each one does, how far it has got, and which claims you can trust.

Short answerMost GTA 6 PC port projects build on VIctory, published November 23, while XCL is a separate compatibility layer and ScarlettPort is the first port with a public release. The furthest builds reach the open world and the first missions at about 20 FPS at best, with most in single digits. None is stable or properly playable, and as of November 27 there is no Take-Two takedown notice against VIctory or XCL, though Leonida and 3,007 repositories were removed on November 24.

One family tree, plus one rival

The first thing to understand about the GTA 6 PC port race is that most of the projects are the same code with different names. HotRepo, which compared 12 projects from a snapshot taken at 12:12 UTC on November 27, puts it plainly: VIctory is the base of most of the projects above. It was published on November 23 by Marvisak, and it runs the games own code on Windows, supplies the consoles system services, and translates its graphics programs. Within two days, at least five public repositories carried on its first commit, and most of the projects in the comparison use its work.

The exception is XCL. It is a separate line, started by ivall on November 20, and it is a general Xbox Series compatibility layer rather than a GTA 6 project. Its readme lists Minecraft Preview and Unicorn Overlord as fully playable, and it does not name GTA 6 at all. It says only that a newer retail game reaches its 3D world on a test branch, at about 5 FPS on an RTX 5090. XCL had 8,663 stars in the November 27 snapshot, and it is the project behind the race victory story our site covered earlier this week. Our XCL race report has that background.

Then there is Leonida, the project that started it all. It opened on November 20, passed 32,000 stars in three days, and stopped on the evening of November 23, when its repository addresses went dead and its Discord server disappeared. Take-Two published a takedown notice the next day, and GitHub disabled its network of 3,007 repositories. A continuation project, Leonida Continued, carries on its commit history and had 2,584 stars on November 27, but its readme shows menus and story loading with no verified world frame. The original Leonida never had a playable version.

HotRepo groups the 12 projects by the stage their readmes support, and warns that each row is that projects own test on its own card, scene and settings, so the frame rates cannot be compared across rows. A few rows deserve a closer look. klbf-lab is the only project that follows a shared measurement method, using the same save and the same route, and its 16.9 FPS average comes from a full 60 minute test with no crash. VIctory-NVIDIA looks fastest on paper at 26 to 30 FPS, but that is in the opening rooms at 1440p with DLSS Quality, and its releases include an unsigned executable. AnyXboxX reports about 24 FPS median at 1080p and 30 FPS in light scenes, but with ray tracing off, which leaves indoor and shadowed areas dark. Victory-fork, at 3,320 stars, is the most starred fork, but its graphics card is lost 30 to 50 minutes into a session. And the original VIctory, the base of them all, runs at about 1 FPS on an RX 6700 XT. The base is slow; the forks are where the speed is.

ScarlettPort: first public release, not the fastest

ScarlettPort deserves its own paragraph, because it is the project most people will actually try. DSOGaming reported on November 27 that version 0.5.2 is the first unofficial GTA 6 PC port with a public release, which means it can be run directly instead of built from source. It boots GTA 6, passes the menus and legal screens, and reaches the game world, with a median frame rate of 18.9 to 22.0 FPS on an AMD Radeon RX 9060 XT. It also adds a PC-style mouse and keyboard layout, direct DualSense and DualShock 4 input, and a launcher with an updater.

Its own readme is more modest, reporting about 7 to 8 FPS using the Series S picture profile on an RTX 3060, with haze indoors and no long session recorded yet. Both figures are honest, and the gap between them is the whole story of this comparison: different card, different picture profile, different scene. Our full ScarlettPort report covers version 0.5.2 in detail, and our FPS explainer breaks down why the numbers disagree.

The Mac projects

Two projects are trying to bring the game to Apple Silicon Macs, and both moved on November 27. MacBox uses CrossOver, Rosetta 2 and D3DMetal to bring the Xbox compatibility layer to macOS, and its developer confirmed that GTA 6 rendered its launch animation and reached the terms and conditions screen on an M5 Max Mac. A separate macOS build, VIctory-macOS, reported about 2 to 3 FPS in a November 27 update, and its readme says the world loads on several Macs, with the M2 Pro taking about 2 seconds per frame and the M4 Pro about 0.71 seconds per frame. The Mac Observer, which reported both updates, is clear that neither is a playable game. Our November 27 port update covers the Mac news alongside the Windows race.

The private builds and the ones that vanished

Not every project in the race is public. CGBJ is a private build that the tracker PortWatch VI says is based on VIctory with some of XCLs work. Its code is not public, so nobody outside can test or reproduce it, and HotRepo found no public release and says download links circulating online cannot be verified. DSOGaming adds that a build claiming to be CGBJ is already on torrent sites, and advises avoiding it, since torrent sites are currently filled with fake GTA 6 PC torrents. A clip claiming 30 FPS multiplayer on a CGBJ build circulated on November 27, but it is unconfirmed, and our multiplayer video explainer has the full picture.

Two projects dropped out of the comparison this week. ZETTROX, which Notebookcheck called the top-ranked build at about 2 FPS, was removed on November 26 after its authors whole account stopped loading, with no public takedown notice naming it. VIctory-Belobug-AMD was also removed, after its readme was replaced with VIctory-7900XTXs, so it no longer shows progress of its own. Projects in this race can vanish overnight, with or without a notice.

What it takes to run any of them

The hardware demands are the same across the family, because the code is largely the same. VIctorys readme asks for Windows 10 or 11, a processor with AVX2 and a Direct3D 12 card with shader model 6.6, plus mesh shaders for the game world. On usage, VIctory takes about 33 GB of committed memory and about 10 GB of video memory in the world, victory-forks test takes 23 to 27 GB, AnyXboxX takes 20 to 25 GB with 32 GB recommended, and VIctory-7900XTXs tests take 37 to 45 GB. To follow the race as it moves, the comparison points to two trackers: GTA VI PC Race, which reads the public repositories every 15 minutes, and PortWatch VI, which scores builds by its own standard. Our PortWatch VI tracker guide explains how that scoring works.

As of 12:12 UTC on November 27, HotRepo found no Take-Two notice against VIctory or XCL in GitHubs takedown archive. That is a fact about today, not a promise about tomorrow. Leonida was the biggest project in the race until it stopped on November 23, and its network of 3,007 repositories was gone the next day. The projects compared here are unofficial, built on files leaked from the Xbox version, and none of them is stable, finished or properly playable. If you want to play GTA 6 on PC today, the safe route is still Remote Play from your own console, and the only real answer to when a PC version arrives is the one Rockstar has not given yet.

Quick answers

Which GTA 6 PC port project is the furthest along?

Several builds have reached the open world and the first missions, including klbf-lab, VIctory-NVIDIA, Vice-Tay, VIctory-7900XTX, victory-fork and AnyXboxX. The fastest logged results are around 20 FPS in light scenes, and none is stable or properly playable. ScarlettPort is the furthest in a different sense: it is the first with a public release.

What is the difference between VIctory and XCL?

VIctory is the base project most ports build on. It was published on November 23 and runs the games own code on Windows. XCL is a separate line started on November 20, a general Xbox Series compatibility layer whose readme does not name GTA 6 at all. Its author says a newer retail game reaches its 3D world at about 5 FPS on an RTX 5090.

Is ScarlettPort the best GTA 6 PC port?

It is the most accessible, because version 0.5.2 is the first public release, so it can be run without building it from source. On speed it is mid pack: its own readme reports about 7 to 8 FPS on an RTX 3060, while tests cited by DSOGaming show a median of 18.9 to 22.0 FPS on an RX 9060 XT. Other projects report faster numbers in easier scenes.

Are any of these projects safe to download?

Be careful. None of them distributes game files, and they all need files from the leaked Xbox build. Most files circulating as a GTA 6 PC download carry malware, and builds claiming to be the private CGBJ project are already on torrent sites. We do not link to downloads. Take-Two removed Leonida and 3,007 repositories on November 24, and the same enforcement can reach any project.

Has Take-Two taken down VIctory or XCL?

Not as of 12:12 UTC on November 27, according to HotRepo, which checked GitHubs takedown archive and found no Take-Two notice against either project. That can change at any time. Leonida stopped on November 23 and its network of 3,007 repositories was removed the next day.
