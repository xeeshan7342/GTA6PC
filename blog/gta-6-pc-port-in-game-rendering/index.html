GTA 6 reaches in-game rendering on PC

By GTA6 PC EditorialPublished November 25, 2026

The unofficial race to run GTA 6 on PC took its biggest step yet on November 24, 2026. One day after the missing Xbox executable leaked, the compatibility project VIctory moved past the menus and started rendering the game world, and a private build called CGBJ reportedly reached actual in-game movement. The progress is real, and so are its limits: the game runs at about 1 frame per second, most of the scene is missing, and nothing here is playable.

Short answerUnofficial PC port projects have moved past the menus. VIctory now renders in-game graphics from the leaked Xbox build, with menus at 30 fps and in-game frames at roughly 0.6 to 2 fps. A private build called CGBJ reportedly goes further, with a player moving and talking to NPCs. There is still no verified playable build of GTA 6 on PC.

What happened on November 24

On November 24, 2026, TweakTown reported that unofficial GTA 6 PC projects had moved beyond the menus, with newly shared footage showing early 3D environments and partially working shaders. The step came just one day after Cyberleek released the missing executable for the leaked Xbox build on November 23, the file modders needed before any of the game's code could run on Windows at all.

XDA Developers reported the same day that VIctory, the most promising of the new projects, had not only booted the game on PC but was rendering in-game graphics. Clips shared on social media showed the game booting, menus running at 30 frames per second, and some of the early cutscenes rendering. XDA noted it had not independently verified the clips.

That is a genuine step past where the race stood a day earlier, when modders had only reached the settings screens. Our boot report covers that first milestone, and our port race report covers the projects that picked up the work after Leonida shut down.

The numbers from VIctory's test

According to VIctory's November 23 development update, reported by TweakTown, testing on a Windows 11 PC with an AMD Radeon RX 6700 XT graphics card showed menus running at 30 frames per second, working save functionality, and intro video playback with audio. The project loads into the game world in about 50 seconds and reaches the first mission's introductory sequence.

Past that point, the picture changes sharply. Most of the scene still fails to render, and individual frames take between 0.5 and 1.8 seconds to draw, which works out to roughly 0.6 to 2 frames per second. Entering the game world can also cause GPU hangs and display driver resets. In other words, the game loads, but it does not play.

VIctory works by running the Xbox build's original x86-64 code directly on Windows, reimplementing the console-only functions the game calls, and translating its GPU shaders into DirectX Intermediate Language (DXIL) for Direct3D 12. That is the same idea as Leonida in spirit, but Leonida translated shaders to SPIR-V for Vulkan instead. The shared x86-64 processor design between Xbox Series X|S and modern PCs is why this progress came so fast: there is no processor to emulate, only console graphics and system functions to rebuild.

CGBJ: the private build that went further

DSOGaming reported on November 24 that a private build called CGBJ has gone further than any public project. CGBJ is based on VIctory and uses some work from XCL, and it reportedly reaches in-game: a player can move with a controller, talk to NPCs, and open the in-game menu.

The limits are still severe. PortWatch lists the build at about 4 frames per second, with no lighting yet, and the graphics are flat-colored and mostly untextured, according to the tracker that XDA cites for the claim. DSOGaming itself urges readers to take the report with a grain of salt, noting that PortWatch has reportedly confirmed it but that the footage could still be fake. A separate clip shared on Reddit, showing partially working shaders, character models and environmental rendering, comes from yet another private project, according to TweakTown, and may eventually be folded into one of the public projects or released on its own.

CGBJ is private and cannot be downloaded. There are no public releases of VIctory or XCL either, though both projects are open source and can be built from their GitHub pages by people who want to try.

Leonida, the static recompilation project that led the race for its first three days, is no longer in active development after its developer stepped away and its pages went down. Our crackdown report has the full story.

How Portwatch scores the race

Portwatch is a live tracker that scores every project on one shared scale. TweakTown reports VIctory at 67 percent, and the leading project at 72 percent. The scale is not linear: 55 percent means the main menu, 70 percent means in-game, 85 percent means 30 fps, and 100 percent means stable. The jump from in-game to a stable 30 fps is the long part of the race, and the tracker notes the last stretch will need much more development time. Our Portwatch guide explains how to read the tracker and what each milestone means.

Why it still is not playable

Despite the headlines, TweakTown's conclusion is blunt: no verified, fully playable public build of GTA 6 for PC exists. The public projects crash when they try to reach the game world, and the private build that gets there runs at about 4 frames per second with no lighting. The game also has a watchdog timer that ends the process roughly 30 seconds after the world loads, which the projects are still working around.

The next milestone is not a higher number on a tracker. It is correct, artifact-free visuals at a frame rate a person could actually play at, and DSOGaming says that is now the big question for every project in the race.

The dump problem nobody has solved

There is still no known way for Xbox Series X|S owners on up-to-date firmware to extract their own decrypted copy of GTA 6, according to XDA. All of the port work is being done on the leaked copy, which means these projects cannot grow into something ordinary players can use legally. Anyone offering a ready-made download is either sharing leaked files or, more often, running a scam. Our download scams guide explains the risks in detail.

What to watch next

DSOGaming expects XCL and VIctory to close on CGBJ within hours or days, because the projects share work and crash at the same wall. Watch for a public project reaching the game world without crashing, for lighting and textures appearing in footage, and for the frame rate climbing out of single digits. Until then, every "GTA 6 on PC" video deserves the same check: is it on the tracker, is there code behind it, and has anyone independent confirmed it?

Do not download anything claiming to be GTA 6 for PC. There is no legitimate download, and fake installers using the leak as bait are already circulating. The unofficial port projects are open-source code on GitHub, not game downloads, and they need leaked files to run.

Quick answers

Has GTA 6 reached in-game on PC?

Reportedly, yes, in a limited sense. VIctory renders in-game graphics, and the private build CGBJ reportedly lets a player move and talk to NPCs. Most of the scene is missing, the frame rate is about 1 fps, and nothing is playable.

What is VIctory?

VIctory is an open-source compatibility layer that runs the Xbox Series X|S build of GTA 6 on Windows. It executes the game's original x86-64 code directly and reimplements console system functions while translating GPU shaders to DirectX Intermediate Language (DXIL).

What is CGBJ?

CGBJ is a private build based on VIctory, using some work from the XCL project. It reportedly reaches in-game, with controller movement, NPC interaction and an open in-game menu. It is not public and cannot be downloaded.

Can I play GTA 6 on PC now?

No. No project has a verified playable build. The furthest public progress is a loaded world that renders at roughly 0.6 to 2 frames per second, with most of the scene missing and frequent GPU hangs.

Is it safe to download a GTA 6 PC port?

No. There is no legitimate GTA 6 PC download. Files claiming to be a working port are usually fake installers or malware, and the leaked build itself carries legal risk. Do not download anything claiming to be GTA 6 for PC.
