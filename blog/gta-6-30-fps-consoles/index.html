GTA 6 runs at 30 FPS on consoles. Here's why PC matters

By GTA6 PC EditorialPublished November 24, 2026

GTA 6 looks incredible on consoles, but it runs at 30 frames per second on all of them. That's one of the biggest reasons PC players are waiting for their version.

Short answerRockstar targets 30 FPS on every console, including PS5 Pro, with no 60 FPS mode. Digital Foundry found PS5 Pro the steadiest, base PS5 and Series X dipping into the 20s, and Series S rendering around 720p. A PC version should allow higher frame rates on strong hardware.

Rockstar's 30 FPS target

In its November 10, 2026 post, Rockstar said GTA 6 targets 30 FPS on current consoles, including the PS5 Pro. It hasn't announced a 40 or 60 FPS mode. Earlier rumors that the PS5 Pro would hit 60 FPS turned out to be wrong.

How each console performs

Digital Foundry tested the game for more than 100 hours. Here's what it found:

ConsoleResolutionFrame rate
PS5 Pro~1440p, upscaled toward 4K with PSSR, plus ray-traced shadowsHolds ~30 FPS, brief dips to ~24 in big explosions
PS5~1440p30 FPS, often high 20s in combat, low 20s at worst
Xbox Series X~1440pSimilar to PS5
Xbox Series S~720p, upscaled to ~1080p with FSRThe hardest version to recommend

Why it's capped at 30

GTA 6 pushes a huge, dense open world with detailed lighting, crowds and traffic. On consoles, Rockstar chose image quality and world detail over frame rate. That's a common trade-off for big open-world games, and Rockstar made the same choice with Red Dead Redemption 2.

What this means for PC

On PC, Rockstar's past games let players choose. Both GTA V and Red Dead Redemption 2 on PC supported unlocked frame rates and high refresh rate monitors. If GTA 6 follows suit, a strong gaming PC should be able to run it well above 30 FPS, with modern upscaling like DLSS or FSR helping further.

There's a catch: if the game is this demanding on a PS5 Pro, expect the PC version to need serious hardware for 60 FPS and above. See our estimated PC requirements.

Is it worth waiting for PC for the frame rate?

If smooth performance matters most to you and you have a capable PC, probably yes. If you own a PS5 Pro and just want to play, it's the best console version. We weigh it up in Wait for PC or play on console?
