GTA 6 multiplayer on a PC port: the viral video explained, and what it does not prove

By GTA6 PC EditorialPublished November 27, 2026

The unofficial GTA 6 PC port race reached a new claim on November 26, 2026: multiplayer. A short video shared on X shows two characters walking down a neon street in the same world, with a chat overlay and a voice saying the game is online before any stable version exists. It passed 120,000 views within hours. The build behind it is private, nobody outside the project has tested it, and the clip proves far less than its headline suggests.

Short answerOn November 26, 2026, a video showed two players moving around the same world in an unofficial GTA 6 PC build, with cars and proximity voice chat, reportedly at 25 to 30 frames per second. The footage comes from a developer called TeK, working on a private fork of the CGBJ build. The community tracker PortWatch rates the clip as real, but no outlet has independently verified it, there is no public download, and this is not Rockstar's GTA Online. Treat it as an unverified claim from a private build.

What the video actually shows

The clip is short and, on its face, unremarkable, which is part of why it spread. Two characters walk down a street in the game's open world while a chat overlay reads, in the poster's own spelling, that they got GTA 6 online before a stable version exists. A voice says much the same thing. There is no post body beyond the title. The video is the whole argument.

The timeline is clear. The post landed on Reddit's r/singularity at 6:39 on Thursday evening, November 26, 2026, and by midnight it was the subreddit's most discussed video of the day, with about 1,700 upvotes and more than 430 comments. The poster, u/141_1337, linked two posts on X as the source of the footage. The more consequential of the two, according to the community tracker Leonida Hub, is a video posted on November 26 by a developer known as TeK, showing multiplayer running on a private fork of the CGBJ build, with player characters, cars and proximity voice chat at 25 to 30 frames per second. The X post itself passed 120,000 views quickly.

Why nobody can verify it

Every claim in this story rests on one video from code nobody else can run. The build is private, the code has not been shared, and no outlet has independently verified the footage. The trackers are careful about this: PortWatch rates the clip as real, according to Leonida Hub, but that is a tracker judgement based on the footage, not an independent test. The most upvoted comments on the Reddit thread are reactions, not confirmations.

That matters because multiplayer is a much bigger claim than a frame rate. Getting two players into the same world means syncing positions, animations, vehicles and voice across a network, on top of a port that already struggles to hold 20 frames per second in single player. The fastest community builds run at about 20 fps with missing water and broken lighting, as our November 26 update covers, and the main VIctory project still lists its first mission intro at 1 to 2 seconds per frame. A private fork claiming 25 to 30 fps with multiplayer attached is exactly the kind of claim that needs a public build before anyone should believe the number.

The fake video that came first

The port race has already taught this lesson once. On launch day, November 19, an X account called @recompdev posted a video of GTA 6's opening titles supposedly running on a PC, with a performance overlay in the corner. XDA Developers took it apart the same day: the title screen was stitched onto footage from an older video of the PS5 version of GTA 5 running on PC, the overlay appeared to loop, and the account was new. The clip was a hoax, and it went viral anyway.

The new multiplayer clip has no such smoking gun. Nobody has shown it to be spliced footage, and the tracker community treats it as plausible. But plausible is not verified, and the difference matters. A video can show two characters in a world without proving they are connected over a network, and it can show a chat overlay without proving the voice chat works. Until the code is public or an independent tester runs it, the clip sits in the same category as the 25 fps CGBJ claim: interesting, consistent with the direction of the work, and unproven.

What real GTA Online on PC would look like

It helps to separate this video from the thing most readers actually want. GTA Online is Rockstar's official multiplayer mode, with servers, accounts, progression and anti-cheat. It cannot exist on PC until GTA 6 itself exists on PC, and Rockstar has announced no PC version. Our GTA 6 Online on PC page tracks the official position, which is still silence, and the rumoured early 2027 window remains a rumour.

What TeK's video claims is something else entirely: two players synced inside an unofficial port built from leaked Xbox files, with no Rockstar servers involved. Even if the clip is genuine, that is a tech demo inside a broken build, not a game mode. There is no lobby, no progression, no anti-cheat, and no way for anyone else to join. The first mod menu and launcher for these builds appeared only yesterday, November 26, and both are debugging tools, not something you can download and play.

The bottom line

November 26 ended with the port race claiming its flashiest milestone yet: multiplayer, before the game is stable, before it is playable, and before it officially exists on PC at all. The video is real footage of something, the tracker community finds it credible, and the technical leap it implies is large. But the build is private, the frame rate is self-reported, and the last viral video in this race turned out to be a hoax built from GTA 5 footage. Watch the clip, enjoy the idea of it, and download nothing. If a public build ever appears, that is when the claim becomes testable.

Quick answers

Is GTA 6 multiplayer working on PC?

Not in any way you can play. A video posted November 26, 2026 shows two players in an unofficial PC build, but the build is private, the code has not been shared, and no outlet has independently verified the clip. There is no official PC version and no public multiplayer build.

What does the viral GTA 6 multiplayer video show?

Two characters walk down a neon street in the game's open world while a chat overlay reads that the game is online before a stable version exists, and a voice says the same. The footage is attributed to a developer called TeK, working on a private fork of the CGBJ build, with proximity voice chat at a reported 25 to 30 frames per second.

Who made the multiplayer video?

The clip was posted on X on November 26, 2026 and then shared to Reddit by u/141_1337, where it drew about 1,700 upvotes and more than 430 comments. The footage itself is attributed by the Leonida Hub tracker to a developer known as TeK, working on a private fork of the CGBJ port build.

Is this the same as GTA Online on PC?

No. GTA Online is Rockstar's official multiplayer mode, and it has no PC version because GTA 6 itself has no PC version. The video shows two players synced in an unofficial port built from leaked Xbox files. It has nothing to do with Rockstar's servers, accounts or anti-cheat.

Could the multiplayer video be fake?

It could be, and that is why it is labelled unverified. The port race has already produced one viral fake: a November 19 clip claiming a PC port was exposed by XDA Developers as GTA 6 title footage spliced onto older GTA 5 footage, with a looping performance overlay. The new clip has no such smoking gun, but a single video from a private build is not verification.
