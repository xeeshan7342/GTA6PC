GTA 6 PC port update, Nov 27: ScarlettPort goes public as MacBox reaches an M5 Max

By GTA6 PC EditorialPublished November 27, 2026

November 27 was the busiest day yet in the race to get GTA 6 running on PC. ScarlettPort became the first unofficial port with a public release, an experimental Mac project rendered the game on an M5 Max, and a clip claiming 30 FPS multiplayer spread across social media. At the same time, Take-Two's takedowns kept biting, with 3,007 repositories already removed. Here is everything that changed today, and what is still unconfirmed.

Short answerOn November 27, 2026, ScarlettPort 0.5.2 became the first unofficial GTA 6 PC port with a public release, reaching the open world at about 20 FPS. The Mac project MacBox rendered GTA 6's launch animation on an M5 Max Mac, while a separate macOS build runs at 2 to 3 FPS. A clip claiming 30 FPS multiplayer on PC is unconfirmed. Take-Two has had 3,007 repositories taken down, and no port is stable or safe to download.

The day in short

Four things happened on November 27 that move the port race forward, and one that holds it back. ScarlettPort 0.5.2 became the first unofficial GTA 6 PC port with a public release. MacBox rendered GTA 6 on an M5 Max Mac. A separate macOS build, VIctory, reported 2 to 3 FPS. And a clip claiming 30 FPS multiplayer on PC spread across social media, unconfirmed. Holding it all back is Take-Two's enforcement: a DMCA notice published by GitHub on November 24 took down Project Leonida and its network of 3,007 repositories, and that pressure has not eased.

The independent tracker puts the state of the race plainly. About 15 projects have got the game world on screen, and in at least 6 of them you can control the character. The best credible frame rate is around 20 FPS, on the most powerful cards, in light scenes. In the city it drops to 6 to 13 FPS. Most videos showing 60 FPS or 4K at 120 FPS are not the game running on PC at all, but console footage passed through a PC.

ScarlettPort goes public

The biggest news of the day is ScarlettPort. DSOGaming reports that version 0.5.2 is the first unofficial GTA 6 PC port with a public release, which means it can be run directly instead of built from source. It boots GTA 6, passes the menus and legal screens, and reaches the game world, with a median frame rate of 18.9 to 22.0 FPS on an AMD Radeon RX 9060 XT. Version 0.5.2 also adds a PC-style mouse and keyboard layout with mouse look, direct DualSense and DualShock 4 input, and a launcher that shows live shader progress to 100 percent.

Our full report has the details, the fixes in 0.5.2, and the warnings that come with it. Read it here: GTA 6 PC port ScarlettPort goes public. The short version is that this is the furthest a port has come, and it is still a long way from a game you can actually play. It is built on leaked files, it crashes, and Take-Two can take it down.

MacBox reaches an M5 Max Mac

The second story of the day is on Mac. The Mac Observer reports that an experimental project called MacBox uses CrossOver, Rosetta 2 and D3DMetal to bring the Xbox compatibility layer to macOS. In a development update, the developer confirmed that GTA 6 successfully rendered its launch animation and reached the terms and conditions screen on an M5 Max Mac. A separate VIctory macOS development update on November 27 reported performance of around 2 to 3 FPS.

That is a proof of concept, not a playable game, and it is worth saying so plainly. Two to 3 FPS is a slideshow, and a title screen is not the open world. But it is the first time the game has rendered on Apple Silicon at all, and it shows the port race is no longer Windows only. Our is GTA 6 on Mac guide has the full status, including the only legitimate way to play on a Mac today, and the route a future official PC version could open.

Multiplayer clip claims 30 FPS, unconfirmed

TweakTown reported on November 27 that the unofficial PC port now has a kind of multiplayer running at 30 FPS with two players in the world. DSOGaming adds that modders are experimenting with a multiplayer mode on the closed beta build of CGBJ, and a clip from modding group DEG Mods shows multiple players and their cars in one world, with proximity voice chat claimed. Social posts the same day called it GTA 6 multiplayer running on PC before GTA Online even exists.

None of this is verified. Our multiplayer video explainer from November 26 covers the earlier TeK clip in detail, and the same caution applies here: a clip is not a test. Until an outlet verifies two players in one world with independent measurements, treat the 30 FPS multiplayer claim as unconfirmed. The history of this race is full of clips that turned out to be spliced console footage.

Where the race stands

Pulling the day together, the race has three tiers now. At the top is ScarlettPort, the only public release, at about 20 FPS. Below it are the build-it-yourself projects like CGBJ, which reached 91 percent completion at 25 to 30 FPS on November 26, according to our November 26 update, but which is still not public. At the bottom are the experiments like MacBox, rendering title screens at single-digit frame rates. All three tiers share the same limits: leaked files, crashes, and the threat of a takedown.

The private builds are worth a mention too. Our private builds report from today covers the closed projects that never publish their code, and our takedowns report covers exactly what Take-Two removed and why. Read together with this update, they give the full picture of a race that is moving fast and living on borrowed time.

Fakes are still the biggest risk

The most important fact of the day has nothing to do with frame rates. A build claiming to be CGBJ is already on torrent sites, and DSOGaming advises avoiding it, since torrent sites are currently filled with fake GTA 6 PC torrents. The tracker agrees: most files going around as a GTA 6 PC download carry password-stealing malware, and none of the real projects distributes a finished game.

The rule has not changed since launch day. If a site offers you a complete GTA 6 PC download, a single installer, or a torrent of the full game, it is a fake. The real projects contain no game assets and need a copy of the game to run at all. Do not download anything, do not pay anyone, and do not enter your passwords anywhere that promises early access. Rockstar has announced no PC version, and the GamesBeat report pointing to the first quarter of 2027 remains unconfirmed. When there is a real PC version, Rockstar will announce it, and we will cover it here.

What to watch next

Three things matter now. First, whether CGBJ goes public, and whether it beats ScarlettPort's 20 FPS when it does. Second, whether the multiplayer claim gets verified by an outlet that tests it properly. Third, whether Take-Two's enforcement reaches ScarlettPort, as it reached Leonida. Any one of those could change the picture overnight, and we will update this page when it happens. Until then, the honest summary of November 27 is this: the ports are real, they are improving fast, and none of them is a game you can play yet.

Quick answers

What changed in the GTA 6 PC port race on November 27?

ScarlettPort 0.5.2 became the first unofficial GTA 6 PC port with a public release, reaching the open world at about 20 FPS. MacBox rendered GTA 6 on an M5 Max Mac, a separate macOS build hit 2 to 3 FPS, and a clip claiming 30 FPS multiplayer circulated online. Take-Two's November 24 takedown of 3,007 repositories is still in effect.

Can I play GTA 6 on PC now?

Not in any stable way. The best ports reach the open world at around 20 FPS with frequent crashes, and none has demonstrated a stable, fully playable version. Rockstar has announced no official PC version, so the only safe route is to wait for one.

What is MacBox?

MacBox is an experimental project that brings the Xbox compatibility layer to macOS using CrossOver, Rosetta 2 and D3DMetal. On November 27 its developer confirmed GTA 6 rendered its launch animation and reached the terms and conditions screen on an M5 Max Mac. It is a proof of concept, not a playable game.

Is the 30 FPS multiplayer video real?

Unconfirmed. A clip circulating on November 27 claims two-player multiplayer on PC at 30 FPS, and TweakTown reported it the same day, but no outlet has verified it with independent testing. Treat it as a claim, not a fact, until a reputable source confirms it.

Is it safe to download a GTA 6 PC port?

No. Most files circulating as a GTA 6 PC download carry password-stealing malware, and builds claiming to be the CGBJ port are already on torrent sites. Take-Two is actively removing these projects. We do not link to downloads.
