Take-Two confirms the DMCA takedown that shut down Leonida

By GTA6 PC EditorialPublished November 24, 2026Updated November 25, 2026

Update, November 25, 2026: the shutdown is now confirmed as Take-Two's doing. The publisher filed a DMCA takedown notice with GitHub on November 24, and GitHub removed the Leonida repository and its whole fork network, 3,007 repositories in total. The notice says the project reproduced copyrighted parts of GTA 6 and broke Rockstar's ban on reverse engineering. Leonida's developer has stepped away, its 180,000 member Discord server is closed, and other port projects keep working in public with a target on their backs.

Short answerUpdate, November 25, 2026: Take-Two has confirmed it shut down Leonida with a DMCA notice filed November 24. GitHub removed the project and its fork network, 3,007 repositories in total. The notice calls Leonida a derivative work that breaks Rockstar's ban on reverse engineering. Leonida's developer has stepped away, its 180,000 member Discord server is closed, and other port projects keep working in public. There is still no legitimate way to play GTA 6 on PC.

Update, November 25: Take-Two confirms the takedown

It is now confirmed. Take-Two Interactive filed a DMCA takedown notice with GitHub on November 24, 2026, and GitHub published the notice in its public DMCA archive. The notice, filed by Take-Two's IP Enforcement Team, targets the Leonida repository and more than 2,900 forks of it. Because the fork network was larger than 100 repositories and Take-Two swore that most of the forks infringed in the same way, GitHub removed the whole network at once: 3,007 repositories in total, including the main project. TweakTown, which first reported the confirmation, notes this was the fifth Take-Two takedown over Grand Theft Auto posted to GitHub's public archive in a week.

The notice fills in the timing. It says stolen game files from an Xbox Series build of GTA 6 were distributed publicly on November 20, 2026, and the Leonida repository opened the next day with the purpose of running the Xbox version on PC. Take-Two's argument is that Leonida reproduces copyrighted parts of GTA 6, is a derivative work of the game, and makes copyright infringement easier. It also cites Section 2.3 of Rockstar's terms of service, which bars players from decompiling, decrypting, reverse engineering or modifying Rockstar's games.

A DMCA notice is a claim by a rights holder, not a court ruling. No court has decided whether Leonida infringed anything, and GitHub did not adjudicate the copyright claim. What the notice settles is who took the project down: it was Take-Two, through GitHub's takedown process, not an unexplained disappearance.

What happened to the developer

Leonida's lead developer, the 15-year-old known as NotRexed, announced on November 24 that he is stepping away from the project, citing circumstances beyond his control. The project's Discord server, with about 180,000 members, was taken offline, and News18 reports he also received a two-year ban from Discord. PC Guide reports he has since posted that people should ignore his other posts, writing that something is coming soon. That post is unverified, it promises nothing specific, and it should not be read as the project returning.

The shutdown also hit the project's public presence beyond GitHub and Discord. Clips of the port work have been disappearing from X, and a YouTube video about the in-game progress was removed following a copyright removal request by Take-Two Interactive. The enforcement is ongoing, and it is aimed at the public traces of the work, not just the code.

Leonida shuts down

Leonida, the open-source static recompilation project trying to get the leaked GTA 6 Xbox build running natively on PC, has shut down. Its main developer, ItsNotRexed, announced on X that he is stepping away from the project, citing circumstances beyond his control. Notebookcheck reports the project's GitHub page is no longer accessible and its Discord server has been taken down. NextGenUpdate reports the same, and adds that the developer said Rockstar Games did interfere with the project.

Who took the GitHub page down is now settled: Take-Two filed the DMCA notice, and GitHub carried out the removal, as the update above explains. Rockstar has still not publicly commented on Leonida or on any of the takedowns. Our Leonida explainer covers what the project was trying to do, and our boot report covers the executable release that set off this round of activity.

Other servers and projects hit

Leonida was not the only casualty. NextGenUpdate reports Project Marvis, another GTA 6 PC port project, also had its Discord channel wiped on the same day. Notebookcheck reports users on Reddit saying Rockstar is targeting several GTA 6 related Discord servers, including claims that a community with around six million members was taken down. Those Reddit claims are unconfirmed, and no official statement backs them up yet.

The timing is not a coincidence. The takedowns came the day after Cyberleek released the missing executable and modders booted the game on PC for the first time. The port work had gone from a distant possibility to a running, if barely working, build in a matter of hours, and the response followed just as fast.

This is part of a pattern

Take-Two and Rockstar have moved against GTA 6 leak communities before. On November 11, the GTA 6 Mapping Project, a community that spent four years charting Leonida from trailers, screenshots and leaks, lost access to its Discord server. Notebookcheck reported at the time that followers suspected Take-Two requested a DMCA takedown, though the companies involved did not confirm the cause.

Take-Two had already used legal tools against the leak itself. In August 2026, the company issued subpoenas to Discord and Microsoft to identify the person behind the Cyberleek leaks. The mapping project's website, State of Leonida, stayed online after its Discord disappeared, and the project later closed its Discord itself after finishing its pre-launch map.

The work continues, out of sight

The shutdowns have not stopped the port work. Notebookcheck reports several other modders are still actively working on getting GTA 6 running on PC, and forks of the VIctory project have now shown a player walking around in the game at about 3 frames per second. TweakTown notes that a successor effort calling itself Leonida Continued does not boot, while VIctory's forks reach the world and the opening missions. NextGenUpdate reports a new project has appeared from a team known in the console developer scene, XCL, and that it is private or invite only for now, apparently because of the alleged takedowns. A website called PortWatch has also appeared, tracking the progress of each port project.

That shift, from public GitHub repos and open Discords to private groups, is the direct result of the crackdown. It also makes the work harder to verify. Claims from private groups cannot be checked the way a public repo can, so expect more unverified claims and more fakes in the days ahead. Our port race tracker follows what the public projects report, with the numbers labelled as self-reported where they are.

What this means for PC players

Nothing about the official PC version changes. Rockstar has still not announced a PC version, and the reported first quarter of 2027 window remains unconfirmed. The unofficial port race may slow down, speed up, or move underground, but it does not create a legitimate way to play GTA 6 on PC today.

Do not download anything that claims to be the Leonida project, the leaked executable or a working GTA 6 PC port. With the public projects gone, fake versions are more likely, not less. Quick answers

Did Take-Two shut down Leonida?

Yes, confirmed. Take-Two's IP Enforcement Team filed a DMCA takedown notice with GitHub on November 24, 2026, and GitHub published the notice in its public DMCA archive. The notice targets the Leonida repository and more than 2,900 forks of it. Rockstar itself has still not commented publicly.
