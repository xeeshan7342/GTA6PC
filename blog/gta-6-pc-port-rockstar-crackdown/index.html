Rockstar cracks down on GTA 6 PC port projects

By GTA6 PC EditorialPublished November 24, 2026

One day after modders booted GTA 6 on PC, the main project behind the unofficial port is gone. Leonida, the open-source static recompilation project, has shut down, its GitHub page is no longer accessible and its Discord server has been taken down. Several other GTA 6 communities have reportedly been hit too, and Rockstar has not said a word about any of it.

Short answerThe open-source Leonida project, which tried to run GTA 6 on PC, has shut down: its developer stepped away, its GitHub page is gone and its Discord server was taken down. Other port projects and GTA 6 Discord servers were reportedly hit the same day. Rockstar has not publicly commented, so exactly who took what down is unconfirmed.

Leonida shuts down

Leonida, the open-source static recompilation project trying to get the leaked GTA 6 Xbox build running natively on PC, has shut down. Its main developer, ItsNotRexed, announced on X that he is stepping away from the project, citing circumstances beyond his control. Notebookcheck reports the project's GitHub page is no longer accessible and its Discord server has been taken down. NextGenUpdate reports the same, and adds that the developer said Rockstar Games did interfere with the project.

Whether Rockstar or Take-Two directly removed the GitHub page is not confirmed. NextGenUpdate notes it remains to be verified whether the GitHub removal was Rockstar's doing, while the developer's own statement points to the Discord takedown. Rockstar has not publicly commented on Leonida or on any of the takedowns. Our Leonida explainer covers what the project was trying to do, and our boot report covers the executable release that set off this round of activity.

Other servers and projects hit

Leonida was not the only casualty. NextGenUpdate reports Project Marvis, another GTA 6 PC port project, also had its Discord channel wiped on the same day. Notebookcheck reports users on Reddit saying Rockstar is targeting several GTA 6 related Discord servers, including claims that a community with around six million members was taken down. Those Reddit claims are unconfirmed, and no official statement backs them up yet.

The timing is not a coincidence. The takedowns came the day after Cyberleek released the missing executable and modders booted the game on PC for the first time. The port work had gone from a distant possibility to a running, if barely working, build in a matter of hours, and the response followed just as fast.

This is part of a pattern

Take-Two and Rockstar have moved against GTA 6 leak communities before. On November 11, the GTA 6 Mapping Project, a community that spent four years charting Leonida from trailers, screenshots and leaks, lost access to its Discord server. Notebookcheck reported at the time that followers suspected Take-Two requested a DMCA takedown, though the companies involved did not confirm the cause.

Take-Two had already used legal tools against the leak itself. In August 2026, the company issued subpoenas to Discord and Microsoft to identify the person behind the Cyberleek leaks. The mapping project's website, State of Leonida, stayed online after its Discord disappeared, and the project later closed its Discord itself after finishing its pre-launch map.

The work continues, out of sight

The shutdowns have not stopped the port work. Notebookcheck reports several other modders are still actively working on getting GTA 6 running on PC. NextGenUpdate reports a new project has appeared from a team known in the console developer scene, XCL, and that it is private or invite only for now, apparently because of the alleged takedowns. A website called PortWatch has also appeared, tracking the progress of each port project.

That shift, from public GitHub repos and open Discords to private groups, is the direct result of the crackdown. It also makes the work harder to verify. Claims from private groups cannot be checked the way a public repo can, so expect more unverified claims and more fakes in the days ahead.

What this means for PC players

Nothing about the official PC version changes. Rockstar has still not announced a PC version, and the reported first quarter of 2027 window remains unconfirmed. The unofficial port race may slow down, speed up, or move underground, but it does not create a legitimate way to play GTA 6 on PC today.

Do not download anything that claims to be the Leonida project, the leaked executable or a working GTA 6 PC port. With the public projects gone, fake versions are more likely, not less. Quick answers

Why did the Leonida GTA 6 project shut down?

Its main developer, ItsNotRexed, announced on November 24, 2026 that he is stepping away from the project, citing circumstances beyond his control. The project's GitHub page is no longer accessible and its Discord server has been taken down.

Did Rockstar shut down Leonida?

Not confirmed. The developer said Rockstar Games did interfere with the project, but Rockstar has not publicly commented, and it remains unverified whether Rockstar was behind the GitHub removal.

Which other GTA 6 projects were taken down?

Project Marvis, another GTA 6 PC port project, also had its Discord channel wiped the same day. Users on Reddit report several GTA 6 related Discord servers were targeted, including claims about a community with around six million members, but those claims are unconfirmed.

Is anyone still working on a GTA 6 PC port?

Yes. Other modders are still working on it, and a new project from a team known in the console developer scene, XCL, has appeared as a private, invite-only group. A website called PortWatch now tracks the progress of each port project.

Can I still download the Leonida project?

No legitimate copy is available from the project's own pages, which are gone. Do not download anything claiming to be Leonida, the leaked executable or a working GTA 6 PC port. Fake versions are likely and can carry malware.
